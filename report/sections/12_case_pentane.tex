\section{Case V: pentane --- coupled torsions and an incomplete reaction coordinate}
\label{sec:case_pentane}

Pentane has two dihedrals, $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$, coupled by the 1--5
Lennard--Jones interaction (the ``pentane effect''; \cref{sec:alkane_model}). We bias
only $\varphi_1$, so $\varphi_2$ is a \emph{hidden} slow coordinate: sampling
$F(\varphi_1)$ correctly requires the conditional law $p(\varphi_2\mid\varphi_1)$ to
equilibrate, which ABF does not directly drive. This is the demanding test of
whether mFR reallocation improves reaction-coordinate support \emph{without}
corrupting the conditional law.

\subsection{Design and reference}
The joint reference $F(\varphi_1,\varphi_2)$ is built by internal-coordinate
free-energy perturbation (\cref{fig_alk_04_pentane_joint}); the one-dimensional
reference $F(\varphi_1)$ is its marginal (\cref{fig_alk_05_pentane_marginal}) and is
barely shifted from $V_4(\varphi_1)$ --- the coupling lives almost entirely in the
\emph{conditional}, not the marginal. This is exactly what makes a ``false
improvement'' possible: a method can flatten the $\varphi_1$ population while
mis-sampling $\varphi_2\mid\varphi_1$. We therefore report, against the joint
reference, per-$\varphi_1$-bin conditional total variation and KL, conditional basin
probabilities over $\{T,\mathrm{G}^+,\mathrm{G}^-\}$, a reference-weighted aggregate
conditional error, and visits to all nine torsional basins. Methods, seeds, budget
and tuning mirror \cref{sec:case_butane}; we run $\beta\in\{1,2\}$ with localized
trans--trans initialization (dispersed nine-basin as a secondary diagnostic).

\subsection{Results}

The central distinction is \emph{healthy} improvement (better $F(\varphi_1)$ with
better-or-unchanged conditional fidelity) versus \emph{false} improvement (flatter
$\varphi_1$ but worse $p(\varphi_2\mid\varphi_1)$). The data give a clear answer, and
it is neither: at $\beta=1$ ABF attains a median final $L_2(F(\varphi_1))$ of
$\AlkPentaneAbfL2$ with a reference-weighted conditional TV of $\AlkPentaneAbfCondTV$;
the deployable (tuned, gentle) mFR attains $\AlkPentaneFrL2$ and $\AlkPentaneFrCondTV$
--- \textbf{practically equivalent on both the marginal and the hidden conditional},
because at the tuned rate the birth--death barely fires (ancestor ESS stays at
$\sim$90\% of the population). The $\varphi_1$ marginal is simply not starved: ABF
flattens it as easily as in butane, so there is no support deficit for mFR to
correct, and the coupling-induced difficulty lives in $\varphi_2$, which mFR does not
bias. The labelled \texttt{fr\_active} probe (rate $\AlkFrActiveRate$) makes the
consequence of forcing the mechanism explicit: it \emph{worsens} the marginal
($0.301$ vs $\AlkPentaneAbfL2$), collapses ancestor ESS to $\sim$180, and at this
primary trans--trans setting also \emph{worsens} the conditional TV
($0.038$ vs $\AlkPentaneAbfCondTV$) --- harm on every axis, not a healthy gain.
\Cref{fig_alk_09_pentane_conditional,fig_alk_10_pentane_basins} show the marginal
accuracy, the hidden-conditional fidelity, and the basin occupancies for every
method. Tuned OPES is markedly worse on the marginal ($\AlkPentaneOpesL2$ vs
$\AlkPentaneAbfL2$) at matched force-evaluation budget --- its deposited bias
under-explores $\varphi_1$ (and at $\beta=2$ misses two of the nine basins) --- while
its conditional is comparable; both its native-reweight and common mean-force
estimates are reported.

\subsection{Rate--diversity behaviour and frozen-bias validation}
\label{sec:alkane_tuning}
\Cref{fig_alk_11_fr_rate_ladder} maps the mFR rate against accuracy and ancestor ESS
on both alkanes. The result is unambiguous and is the same for butane and pentane:
there is \emph{no useful window}. Integrated $L_2(F)$ is smallest at the gentlest
rate and rises monotonically as the rate increases --- always at or above the ABF
baseline --- while ancestor ESS collapses (from $\sim$95\% of the population toward
$\sim$35\% at the strongest rate). For pentane the reference-weighted conditional TV
$p(\varphi_2\mid\varphi_1)$ \emph{also} worsens monotonically with rate, so an
aggressive mFR simultaneously degrades the marginal free energy, corrupts the hidden
conditional, and destroys lineage diversity. The pre-registered selection (smallest
median integrated $L_2(F)$ subject to genealogy) therefore returns the effectively
inactive rate ($\AlkFrRate$), and the labelled \texttt{fr\_active} probe at
$\AlkFrActiveRate$ confirms the harm at production scale with matched seeds and full
conditional diagnostics. A representative
frozen-bias validation freezes the learned $B(\varphi_1)$, runs fresh dynamics with
no updates and no birth--death, and reconstructs
$F_{\mathrm{recon}}=B-\beta^{-1}\log p_B+C$; agreement of the reconstructed profile
with the reference certifies that the learned bias --- not a resampling artifact ---
carries the free-energy information. Where the conditional law is the binding
constraint, the analysis relates the mFR gain to the measured ABF error, the
$\varphi_1$ support, the hidden-conditional error, the transition counts, the
genealogy cost and $\beta$, rather than to the nominal barrier alone.

The frozen-bias reconstruction (\cref{tab:alk_frozen}) confirms that the learned
$B(\varphi_1)$ --- whether from ABF or mFR --- carries the free-energy information:
fresh dynamics under the frozen bias, with no updates and no birth--death, reconstruct
$F(\varphi_1)$ from the biased marginal to an error \emph{well below} the online
mean-force-integral estimate. This both certifies that the equivalence of ABF and
tuned mFR is a property of the learned bias itself (not of the online transient or of
resampling) and localizes the shared $\sim$0.27 online floor
(\cref{rem:alk_floor}) specifically to the finite resolution of the
mean-force estimator: the underlying bias is far more accurate than that floor
suggests, and it is the same for ABF and mFR.
