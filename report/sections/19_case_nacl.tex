\section{A second literature-anchored negative: sodium chloride association in water}
\label{sec:nacl}

Methane (\cref{sec:methane}) closed as ABF-sufficient on a landscape whose barrier is a fraction
of $k_BT$, so one reading of that null is simply that the system was too easy. Sodium chloride
association in explicit water was entered to test that reading. It carries a genuine desolvation
barrier of several $k_BT$, it is the introductory ABF example of a published
tutorial~\citep{Talmazan2025} rather than a system we assembled, and it was designated in the
preregistration as the place where \emph{Q1} --- does Fisher--Rao directionality beat prior
directed selection --- would receive its molecular answer, because the WCA five-arm test of
\cref{sec:wca_q1} had left mFR statistically tied by a count-balancing sham.

It did not answer Q1, and the reason is the result: no cell of the preregistered ladder is
under-established, so no Fisher--Rao arm was ever licensed. What makes the study worth a section
is not the verdict, which repeats methane's, but everything that had to be measured before the
verdict could be trusted --- a gate that cannot fire, a projection that could not fail, and a
null whose weakness was written down a day before the number arrived.

\subsection{Model, engine and reference}

The physical model is the published system extracted verbatim: \ce{Na+}, \ce{Cl-} and
$\NaNwater$ rigid CHARMM TIP3P waters, $\NaSites$ sites in total, CHARMM22 parameters with
Lorentz--Berthelot mixing and no \texttt{NBFIX}. Every force-field constant is read back out of
the assembled OpenMM \texttt{System} rather than transcribed from the paper, which removes an
entire class of silent transcription error at the cost of a build step. Unlike SPC/E, CHARMM
TIP3P hydrogens carry real Lennard-Jones parameters, so all $\NaSites$ sites are both charged and
LJ-active; this forced the single authorised generalisation of the methane engine, in which a
split-path LJ-exclusion assertion specific to SPC/E became a validity flag rather than a raised
exception, with the methane path untouched.

Production is NVT at a volume fixed by our own NPT run --- $L = \NaBoxL$~nm from
$V = \NaBoxV \pm \NaBoxVerr$~nm$^3$, a density of $\NaDensity$~g/cm$^3$ --- so the target measure
is exactly the canonical measure of \cref{sec:abf}. That box is $\NaBoxVsPublished\,\%$
denser than the tutorial's shipped cell, which is an $11$~ps snapshot rather than a converged
mean. Seven deviations from the published protocol are declared rather than discovered, and are
recorded in full in \cref{sec:nacl_impl}; the substantive ones are NVT-at-fixed-volume in place
of a Langevin piston, BAOAB with M-SHAKE/RATTLE in place of leapfrog with SETTLE, and no multiple
timestepping where NAMD evaluates reciprocal PME every second step.

The engine itself is consumed, not forked. Particle-mesh Ewald, Lennard-Jones, constraints and
the integrator are the methane study's deliverable; \texttt{src/nacl/} adds only a model layer.
Agreement with OpenMM is better than $\NaParity$ in relative discrepancy on the potential, its
gradient, the local mean force and the ABF bias force across $\NaParityConfigs$ configurations
spanning contact, barrier, solvent-separated and dissociated geometries with deliberately
distinct hydration structures at the same separation, with PME pinned identically in both engines
so that parity cannot depend on a box-derived default. The minimum-image unambiguity assertion
runs at a margin of $\NaMinImageMargin$ rather than methane's $0.98$, because the published
domain reaches $\NaRhi$~nm against a half-box of about $1.45$~nm. That exposure is the published
setup's own and we inherit it under an explicit gate rather than assume it away.

The reference is constrained thermodynamic integration on the same local mean force ABF
estimates, over $\NaRefGrid$ grid points $\times$ $\NaRefBuilds$ builds $\times$ $\NaRefFamilies$
preparation families $\times$ $\NaRefReplicas$ replicas, $\NaRefProdPs$~ps of production each.
Builds agree to $\NaRefLtwoWorst$~kJ/mol against a $\NaRefSpanKJ$~kJ/mol span, a ratio of
$\NaRefRatio$ against an acceptance threshold of $0.5$, at a cost of $\NaRefHours$~GPU-hours.
Against the published $100$~ns ABF profile our reference agrees to $\NaExtRms$~kJ/mol rms over
the full domain, a comparison dominated by the $0.20$--$0.24$~nm wall region where the published
profile rises steeply; it is reported and never used as a gate, and because both profiles are
computed in the same box it validates the implementation while remaining silent on finite-size
systematics.

\subsection{The landscape, and the state that merges}

The reference reproduces the expected structure: a contact ion pair at $\NaCipR$~nm, a
desolvation barrier of $\NaBarrierKT\,k_BT$ at about $\NaBarrierR$~nm, and a solvent-separated
minimum at $\NaSsipR$~nm lying $\NaDeltaW\,k_BT$ above the contact pair. Beyond the barrier the
landscape is flat to within about $\NaFlatKT\,k_BT$.

The solvent-separated minimum is real but shallow, and it \emph{merges} under the frozen
$2\,k_BT$ rule that defines states throughout this campaign. What every artifact labels
\texttt{SSIP} is therefore the merged outer basin $[\NaOuterLo, \NaOuterHi]$, whose nominal
minimum sits at the domain edge rather than at $\NaSsipR$~nm. We keep the label because it is
what the machine-readable record says, and we flag it here because a reader who assumes
\texttt{SSIP} means the physical solvent-separated well will misread every occupancy statement
that follows. NaCl has one genuine metastable state and one merged outer basin, not two wells.

\subsection{Gate 0: the campaign's soundest baseline}

Gate 0 asks whether the conditional mean force can be trusted at fixed $\xi$ at all, and it leads
the frozen gate order because a failure there means marginal selection provably cannot help: by
\cref{prop:sel_invariance} Fisher--Rao reallocation leaves $p_t(y \mid \xi)$ pointwise invariant,
so a wrong conditional ensemble stays wrong under any amount of it. It is also the cheapest
honest way to end a study, since it falls out of the reference the study needs regardless.

Independently prepared solvent families held at the same $r$ disagree by $\NaGateZeroGlobal$ of
$\overline{|F'|}$ globally and $\NaGateZeroBarrier$ through the desolvation region, on
$\NaGateZeroPts$ of $\NaGateZeroPts$ grid points. This is the best figure in the campaign,
against WCA's $\GzWca$, the gateway's $\GzGateway$, and deca-alanine's failing $\GzDeca$
(\cref{sec:deca}). Following Amendment 9 no numerical threshold is attached to the statistic ---
setting one after seeing $R_{15}$'s value would have fixed $R_{15}$'s verdict retrospectively ---
so the reading is against that calibration ladder rather than against a number.

\subsection{Gate A, and the caveat written before the verdict}

Gate A asks whether the hydration states are visible through $r$. On the preregistered quantity,
$\mathrm{TV}(p(\xi \mid Y = a), p(\xi \mid Y = b))$ with $Y$ the frozen hydration triple
$(n_{\ce{NaO}}, n_{\ce{ClH}}, n_{\rm bridge})$, it passes at $\NaGateAPre$ against a threshold of
$0.30$, with per-descriptor values $\NaGateAnNaO$, $\NaGateAnClH$ and $\NaGateAnBridge$.

A total variation of exactly one is not a strong gate, and we measured why rather than
celebrating it. Across $\NaGridDz$~nm bins the descriptors vary between bins by
$\NaOrthoLo$--$\NaOrthoHi\times$ their variation within a bin. NaCl's hydration is very nearly a
\emph{function} of the ion separation, so Gate A here reports a fact about ion physics rather
than a property of the collective variable. Methane's $n_{\rm gap}$ measures
$\NaMethaneOrtho\times$ on the same statistic and retains a standard deviation of
$\MeNgapSdFixedR$ at fixed $r$.

That difference is the whole content of the weak-null caveat, and it was committed a day before
Gate C was read, precisely so it could not be attached selectively:

\begin{quote}
if NaCl returns ABF-sufficient, the verdict carries the caveat that NaCl has little structure
orthogonal to $r$, so \emph{``mFR had nothing to work with'' is a live alternative to ``mFR was
not needed''}, and the null is correspondingly weaker than methane's. It must not be reported as
a second independent null of equal strength. If NaCl returns establishment-limited, the same fact
makes the licence \emph{stronger}.
\end{quote}

We report it as pre-committed. Methane's null is the informative one, because methane keeps real
solvent structure orthogonal to the coordinate --- there was something for reallocation to act on
and it was still unnecessary. NaCl's null is real but weaker, and the two do not stack into
double the evidence.

\subsection{The ladder, and two cells struck before they ran}
\label{sec:nacl_ladder}

The screen holds total compute fixed at $N \times T = \NaBudgetNs$~ns, the published ABF budget,
and sweeps the number of walkers sharing one bias over $N \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ with
$\NaSeeds$ seeds per cell. The preregistered selection rule is mechanical --- the \emph{smallest}
$N$ passing every gate, never the cell with the largest ABF error --- and the entire map is
reported rather than the winner.

Two cells were struck before any of them ran, on an argument that needs no data. Gate C compares
an integer walker count against $0.5\,Q^*_k$, so its power is set by the expected count
$\lambda_k = N Q^*_k$. Basin targets partition, hence $Q^*_k \leq 1$ identically and
$\lambda_k < N$ for every state. The floor is $\lambda \geq \NaLamFloor$, and therefore
\emph{no cell with $N \leq 16$ can reach it for any state, whatever the sampling.} At $N = 16$
the outer basin misses by $\NaNsixteenMiss$ walkers. Since the selection rule searches
\emph{down} the ladder, it runs directly toward the cells in which the gate cannot fail, and
without this guard ``the smallest $N$ passing every gate'' would have returned a cell whose gate
was arithmetically incapable of firing and reported it as a result. The two cells were dropped,
saving about $\NaGpuDaysSaved$ GPU-days, and the classifiable ladder is
$\{\NaNlo, \NaNhi\}$ at $T = \NaTlo$ and $\NaThi$~ns per walker.

We record one withdrawn justification. The decision to run $N = \NaNlo$ unpacked was originally
also defended on throughput grounds, from an inferred packing penalty that turned out to be a
bound derived from checkpoint spacing rather than a measurement, on a run whose co-tenancy cannot
be reconstructed. That argument is withdrawn and the question is unresolved; the decision stands
on the ground that does not depend on it, namely that a packed run would have spent two thirds of
its budget on cells that cannot be classified at all.

\subsection{Gate B: informative here, vacuous in methane}

Gate B requires persistent first arrival within $0.1\,T$ on at least six of eight seeds. It
passes $\NaGateBhits$ on both states in both cells, and the passing \emph{number} is worthless:
first arrival at the state boundary reads $\NaThitSsip$~ps, which is exactly the trace recording
interval. The published starting configuration sits at $\NaStartR$~nm, $\NaStartAboveMin\,k_BT$
up the barrier rather than at the contact minimum, leaving only $\NaBoundaryDist$~nm to the first
boundary --- a distance crossed ballistically in $\NaBallisticPs$~ps, some four times inside one
ballistic step and five times below the trace resolution. The gate was therefore measuring
resolution, not a discovery rate, and could not have failed. This was established on 2026-08-14,
before the gate was read, and it invalidates SPEC~\S7's declared bias, which had assumed the run
started \emph{at} the contact minimum.

What actually carries ``discovery is fast'' is the supplementary far-threshold statistic: first
arrivals at $0.52$, $0.70$ and $1.00$~nm run at $\NaFarSsipLo$--$\NaFarSsipHi$,
$\NaFarSevenLo$--$\NaFarSevenHi$ and $\NaFarOneLo$--$\NaFarOneHi$ times the exact fastest-of-$N$
ballistic floor, all far under the $\NaThresholdHi$~ps threshold. Discovery is genuinely
diffusive and genuinely fast, and the consequence, stated in advance, is that the regime verdict
rests on Gate C alone.

This is worth setting against methane, where the same gate run the same way is vacuous: methane's
far-threshold arrivals reach only $\MeFarRatioLo$--$\MeFarRatioHi\times$ their floor over a
$\MeDomainWidth$~nm domain, about $\MeBallisticSteps$ ballistic steps wide, so no discovery rate
is resolvable there at any budget. The discriminator is domain width in ballistic steps, not
anything about the method.

\subsection{Gate C: no deficit, and what that licenses}

Gate C calls a state under-established when its occupancy falls below $0.5\,Q^*_k(t)$ for a
contiguous stretch of at least $\NaSpanFrac\,T$ in the second half of the run, against the
bias-aware target $Q^*_k(t) \propto \int_{C_k} \exp[-\beta(F_{\rm ref} - B_t)]$. On the powered
state it fires on $\NaGateCdeficit$ seeds in both cells; \cref{fig:nacl_gate_c}(a) shows the
occupancy ratio itself, every seed of both cells.

The contact pair is \emph{non-binding} in both, at $\lambda = \NaLamCipHi$ and $\NaLamCipLo$
against the floor of $\NaLamFloor$, where the smallest resolvable deficit is $\NaMdeCipHi\,\%$
and $\NaMdeCipLo\,\%$ --- that is, the test has degenerated into asking whether the state happens
to be empty. Following Amendment 12.10 such a state is excluded from the verdict in
\emph{both} directions: it may neither contribute a deficit, which would be counting noise, nor
contribute a pass, which would be arithmetic silence. The outer basin is powered in both cells,
at $\lambda = \NaLamSsipHi$ and $\NaLamSsipLo$, and it carries the verdict.

The contact pair is then cleared separately, by a windowed statistic with the power to say
something: the mean $P/Q^*$ over a sliding window of the required deficit length, with the
standard error taken across independent seeds. Its worst window sits at $\NaCipWinHi$ with a
two-sigma band of $[\NaCipWinHiLo, \NaCipWinHiHi]$ at $N = \NaNhi$ and at $\NaCipWinLo$ with
$[\NaCipWinLoLo, \NaCipWinLoHi]$ at $N = \NaNlo$, both clear of the $0.5$ threshold, and no
single seed-window in either cell falls below $\NaCipWorstSeedLo$. The outer basin is carried
through the same audit as an explicit positive control at $\NaSsipWinHi$ and $\NaSsipWinLo$,
because a check exercised only where it passes cannot be distinguished from one that always
passes.

\begin{remark}[What ``Gate C did not fire'' is worth]
\label{rem:nacl_gatec}
Since no state in the study ever fired the gate, nothing had shown it firing at all. We closed
that by planting stationary walker-conserving deficits in the \emph{real} occupancy traces ---
scaling one state's occupancy and redistributing the removed population over the bias-aware
target so each checkpoint's total is preserved --- and running the unmodified gate. It fires on
$0$ of $8$ seeds at a planted $\NaGateCSilent\,\%$ deficit, which is the shortfall it nominally
tests for, and on $8$ of $8$ at $\NaGateCFires\,\%$. The cause is that at a $50\,\%$ deficit the
mean sits exactly \emph{on} the threshold, so noise lifts about half the checkpoints above it and
the required contiguous run rarely forms. The $\lambda \geq \NaLamFloor$ floor was derived from a
single-checkpoint two-sigma criterion standing in for a contiguous-span test: correct arithmetic
about a quantity the gate does not compute. The analytic figures of
$\NaMdeSsipHi$--$\NaMdeSsipLo\,\%$ therefore \emph{overstate} the gate's sensitivity, and
``Gate C found no deficit'' licenses only that no sustained shortfall of $\NaGateCFires\,\%$ or
more occurred. The methane study found the same effect independently at about $60\,\%$.

The verdict does not rest on that statement. What carries it is the direct bound: over every
second-half state-checkpoint on the powered state --- $\NaCheckpointsHi$ of them at $N = \NaNhi$
and $\NaCheckpointsLo$ at $N = \NaNlo$ --- the minimum $P/Q^*$ is $\NaDirectHi$ and $\NaDirectLo$
with medians $\NaDirectMedHi$ and $\NaDirectMedLo$, bounding the largest \emph{instantaneous}
shortfall anywhere at $\NaShortfallMax\,\%$. The windowed band excludes any sustained deficit
larger than about $\NaWindowedBound\,\%$, roughly $\NaWindowedTighter\times$ tighter than the
contiguity gate could report. We re-measured this against its stated scope rather than carrying
it forward: the final-frame minimum would have been $\NaDirectFinalFrame$, so $\NaDirectHi$ is
genuinely the all-checkpoint value and not a bound silently widened in restatement.
\end{remark}

The planted deficit is stationary, so $\NaGateCFires\,\%$ is a floor on the detection threshold
rather than a characterisation of it: a real establishment failure that decays as the bias fills
in would fire less readily still.

\subsection{The instrument, and a defect in the preregistered design}
\label{sec:nacl_instrument}

Two further calibrations were run after the verdict, and one of them found a design defect that
does not change this result but is inherited by any study running an $N$ ladder at fixed
$N \times T$.

The first sweeps the contiguity span itself. At the preregistered $\NaSpanFrac T$ the gate
detects $\NaFrontierPrereg$ planted $50\,\%$ deficits with zero false positives; at
$0.10\,T$ it detects $\NaFrontierDetect$, still with zero false positives; at $0.02\,T$ it
produces $\NaFrontierFalsePos$ false positives. The preregistered span is thus \emph{dominated}
--- same zero false positives, no detection --- and the efficient frontier sits at
$\NaFrontierSpan T$. That frontier does not transfer between systems: methane showed no false
positives at the span where NaCl shows nine, because the frontier is set by each system's own
occupancy autocorrelation. NaCl's is $\NaTauOcc$~ps in the median, ranging
$\NaTauOccLo$--$\NaTauOccHi$, and it is independent of $N$.

The defect follows from that. Because $N \times T$ is fixed, a span defined as a fraction of $T$
scales as $1/N$: it is $\NaSpanBluntHi$ occupancy correlation times at $N = \NaNhi$ but
$\NaSpanBluntLo$ at $N = \NaNlo$, and would reach roughly $59$ at $N = 8$. The cells of the
ladder are therefore \emph{not compared on equal instrument terms}, and the blunting is worst at
small $N$ --- which is the direction the selection rule searches, and the regime the campaign
exists to find. Measured in absolute time the requirement grows only from about $\NaFrontierPsHi$
to $\NaFrontierPsLo$~ps across the ladder while the prescription doubles, so the prescription
overshoots by a factor that reaches roughly twelve by $N = 8$. This changes nothing here --- no
cell fired at any span, the null is carried by the windowed band, and the two small cells were
struck a priori --- but it is a defect in the preregistered design rather than in its execution.
The fix is to set the span in absolute time from a short pilot, adopted for C60 as Amendment
16.12.

The second calibration asks whether the basins are sampled \emph{inside} or merely correct on
aggregate, since Gate C is basin-integrated and the outer basin spans $88\,\%$ of the domain, so
an internal redistribution preserving the integral would be invisible. Splitting each basin into
quarters, the outer basin is clean in both cells: quarter ratios of $\NaWbSsipQlo$--$\NaWbSsipQhi$,
an integrated ratio of $\NaWbSsipRatioHi$ and $\NaWbSsipRatioLo$, a shape total variation of
$\NaWbSsipTVhi$, and the outermost three grid points holding $\NaWbEdgeOcc\,\%$ of walkers
against $\NaWbEdgeTarget\,\%$ of target --- no wall accumulation. The contact pair shows a larger
shape total variation of $\NaWbCipTV$ and an integrated ratio of $\NaWbCipRatio$, but that is
real structure rather than a jam: its walkers concentrate around the $\NaCipR$~nm minimum and
thin toward the barrier, no quarter in either cell falls below the $0.5$ threshold, and
restricting the basin to the frozen $15\,k_BT$ physical window changes the integrated ratio by
nothing at all. \Cref{fig:nacl_within_basin} shows both basins quarter by quarter.

\begin{remark}[The instrument was wrong first, twice]
Both calibrations shipped a broken version before the reported one, and in both cases the defect
made the instrument \emph{more} reassuring rather than less. The sensitivity tool first
redistributed removed mass proportionally to existing outside counts, which has nowhere to put it
at checkpoints where every walker is inside the basin; the mass was silently dropped, the basin's
share rose to $1.0$ --- the opposite of a deficit --- and it reported that $N = \NaNlo$ never
fires up to a $90\,\%$ planted deficit. It was caught by asserting the per-checkpoint invariant
the docstring had always claimed. The within-basin tool first reported a ratio of $1274$ for a
quarter carrying $0.0004\,\%$ of the target: a ratio needs both arguments' populations, and it
now suppresses the ratio outright where the target has none. Neither defect changed the quantity
it was supposedly qualifying. We also record that the three-scheme agreement later observed on
the redistribution choice is \emph{not} independent evidence: the per-state test is structurally
immune to that choice, because planting sets the state's column before touching anything else.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The pre-committed closure}

Four outcomes were given their meanings in \texttt{CLOSURE\_PRECOMMIT.md}, committed while the
$N = \NaNlo$ cell was at step zero of its run --- after the process started, so the timing is
checkable, and before anything could be read from it. They were: (1) powered outer basin with no
sustained deficit, closing the study as ABF-sufficient with the weak-null caveat attached; (2) a
sustained deficit on at least six of eight seeds, licensing mFR at $N = \NaNlo$ and requiring
$\tau_\perp$, rate calibration and all five production arms; (3) the $N = \NaNhi$ shape with an
inconclusive windowed band, closing more weakly as ``ABF-sufficient on the state that can be
measured, undetermined on the other''; and (4) no state reaching the floor, making the cell
unclassifiable and collapsing the ladder to a single cell. The realised outcome is branch (1),
selected by lookup rather than chosen after seeing the number. The document also fixed what is
\emph{not} permitted on any branch: extending the cell, adding seeds, lengthening $T$, relaxing
the $\lambda$ floor to admit $N = 16$ --- which misses by $\NaNsixteenMiss$ walkers, exactly the
temptation a frozen threshold exists to refuse --- or reporting branch (3) or (4) as if it were
branch (1).

The same document projected $\lambda \approx \NaProjSsipB$ and $\NaProjCipB$ at $N = \NaNlo$
against realised values of $\NaLamSsipLo$ and $\NaLamCipLo$, and we record that agreement at its
true weight, which is very little. Since $\lambda = N Q^*$ and the $N = \NaNhi$ cell had already
fixed $Q^*$, multiplying by $\NaNlo$ is arithmetic; the only empirical content is that $Q^*$
barely moves with $N$. \emph{A projection that could not have failed is the same shape as a gate
that could not fire.} What the pre-commitment earned is the other half of the document: four
meanings fixed before any datum existed, which would have done its work had the projection
missed.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{sec:nacl_impl}

Several of the following cost real time to establish and would otherwise have to be rediscovered.

\paragraph*{The timestep gate was retracted and re-run.} The first dynamics gate reported
$2$~fs failing on equipartition at $\Delta T = 2.67$~K and selected $1$~fs. Its standard errors
were computed as $\mathrm{std}/\sqrt{n}$ over correlated samples and were about $2.2\times$ too
small; read honestly the failure was $2.5\sigma$. The natural hypothesis, that OpenMM's kinetic
energy used a different degree-of-freedom convention, was tested and \emph{refuted} --- the gap
between \texttt{getKineticEnergy} and an explicit $\sum \tfrac12 m v^2$ at the same
degree-of-freedom count is exactly $0.000$~K --- so the retraction is attributed to the error
bars. The replacement rule was frozen before its rerun with the borderline number already known,
and separates a deterministic constraint clause in float64 from a statistical equipartition
clause in the production dtype, with both standard errors obtained by blocking at ten
correlation times, calibrated on a known-truth AR(1) series where two-correlation-time blocks
understate by $26\,\%$ and ten by $6\,\%$. At $\NaDt$~fs the rerun gives $\Delta T = 0.739$~K with
$\sigma = 0.320$~K, and $0.739 + 0.320 \leq 2.0$ passes. Note that this run sits $2.31\sigma$ from
OpenMM and passes anyway: the criterion is an equivalence test against a $2.0$~K tolerance, not a
significance test, and it was deliberately written that way so that foreknowledge of the
borderline value could not be exploited in either direction.

\paragraph*{A performance cliff that was not physics.} An early benchmark reported an eight-fold
throughput collapse at large batch. It was \texttt{torch.compile} exhausting dynamo's recompile
limit and falling back to eager for every subsequent configuration, which is why six consecutive
rows reported an identical rate across four different batch sizes. \emph{A flat identical rate
across batch sizes is not physics.} The file is quarantined under its defect name; its
correctness and trajectory gates remain valid, since those run one shape compiled once. A Triton
kernel passes both correctness gates but was not adopted, being slower than the already
parity-gated tensor path. We note that the re-measured timing artifact the fix commit called for
was never produced, so no valid benchmark file exists on disk.

\paragraph*{Resume verification was mis-specified, not the resume.} The original verifier demanded
cross-process bit-identity of an interrupted-and-resumed run against an uninterrupted one. Two
\emph{identical} full runs in two processes diverge more than the interrupted pair does, because
kernel selection differs per process and the difference amplifies chaotically --- a fact this
campaign had already documented for WCA. The check could not have distinguished a resume defect
from chaos in either direction, and would equally have passed a broken resume on a quieter
system. It was replaced by the two properties that are decidable and together are the whole
contract: an in-process serialization round-trip on the real driver, bit-identical on positions,
velocities, forces, RNG state, accumulators, traces and step index; and the loop's
accumulate/advance ordering, pinned by the deterministic bookkeeping harness. Within-process
determinism supplies the rest.

\paragraph*{Two naming traps, both live.} The reference artifact names both Gate A directions the
wrong way round: the generic-looking \texttt{max\_TV} key holds the \emph{superseded}
spec-transpose at $\NaGateATransposed$, while the verdict is \texttt{preregistered\_max\_TV} at
$\NaGateAPre$. The analysis script read the generic key, so the check that can halt the study
evaluated the wrong direction --- harmless only because both clear $0.30$. The script now raises
on a missing field rather than falling back; renaming the key in the artifact is deferred because
it would mean regenerating an accepted reference on a closed study. Separately, Gate~C's power
block used one token, \texttt{lambda\_min}, for both the policy constant and a measured minimum;
they are now \texttt{lambda\_threshold} and \texttt{lambda\_min\_over\_window}. The general form
is \emph{two correct values for one quantity, where the naming makes the wrong one look
canonical}, and neither a derivation check nor a scope audit finds it --- only asking which key
the prose actually read.

\paragraph*{A check that a cache hit skipped.} The declared-bias check at initialisation sits
inside the population-\emph{build} branch. The $N = \NaNlo$ run reused a cached population to
save eighteen minutes, so the check never ran and its manifest field is null: a check that did
not run, reading as no problem. It was recomputed standalone, and it \emph{fails} --- the
published start sits $\NaStartAboveMin\,k_BT$ above the contact minimum, so SPEC~\S7's declared
bias, which assumed a start at the minimum, does not hold. The general form is that
\emph{any check attached to a build step is silently skipped by a cache hit}. The one-line fix
was deliberately deferred, because editing the sampler mid-campaign risks a launch picking up a
different sampler than the pinned worktree, and ladder homogeneity is worth more than an early
diagnostic fix.

\paragraph*{Pins, and what they do not cover.} All four data-generating runs carry the same
sampler commit, and the $N = \NaNlo$ cell was launched from that same pinned worktree rather than
a new pin. Analysis runs at a \emph{different} commit, because the pinned sampler tree predates
the Gate C power guard, the map-completeness guard and the Gate A transpose fix. The detection
rule is baked into the artifact: a gates report lacking an \texttt{analysis\_provenance} block was
produced by a superseded tree and is not a verdict. We note two residual disagreements between
prose and artifact --- the analysis commit and the realised $N = \NaNlo$ value of $\lambda$,
recorded in prose as $30.75$ against the artifact's $\NaLamSsipLo$ --- and quote the artifact.

\paragraph*{Two incidents worth recording.} A retired autolaunch fired after the amendment that
would have forbidden it, launching a two-GPU split with the pre-amendment device assignment baked
in; it was killed within a minute. \emph{The guard did not fail, the launch went around it: a
permission check only protects launches that call it.} Its output could not have been analysed
in any case, because a four-seed half-block is refused by the full-block requirement. Separately,
a third user landed on the device nine hours into the $N = \NaNlo$ run and cost $\NaContention\,\%$
of wall clock with no effect on the physics, against an in-situ rate of $\NaThroughput$~ns/day
aggregate measured on a verified-idle device. \emph{A preflight is point-in-time}, and no launch
guard can see an arrival nine hours later; co-tenancy was sampled every ten minutes thereafter.

\subsection{What this system contributes}

NaCl is a fourth ABF-sufficient system, reached on a landscape with a genuine
$\NaBarrierKT\,k_BT$ desolvation barrier rather than methane's sub-$k_BT$ one, so the methane null
cannot be dismissed as an artifact of an unusually flat landscape. The ABF baseline already
establishes every state the gates can resolve, and marginal reallocation has no deficit to
repair.

It contributes less than methane does, and the reason was fixed in advance rather than argued
afterwards: with hydration $\NaOrthoLo$--$\NaOrthoHi\times$ more variable across $r$ than within
it, NaCl offers marginal reallocation very little to work with, so ``mFR had nothing to act on''
survives as an alternative to ``mFR was not needed''. It is not a second independent null of
methane's strength.

Its sharpest contribution is methodological, and it is negative. Two of the four gates could not
have failed as instrumented --- Gate B because the boundary sat inside one ballistic step of the
published start, Gate C on the contact pair because $\lambda < 1$ makes the test an emptiness
check --- and the preregistered contiguity span is dominated by a value twice as sensitive at
equal false-positive cost. None of this changes the verdict, which rests on a powered state, a
windowed band and a direct instantaneous bound. All of it changes what a passing gate is worth,
and the practice it recommends is to measure each gate's detection threshold on planted signal in
the real traces before quoting the gate's silence as evidence.

Following the preregistration, no rate calibration was performed, no production arm was run, and
$\tau_\perp$ --- required only downstream of a Gate C deficit --- was stopped and not restarted.
