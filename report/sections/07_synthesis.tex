\section{Cross-case synthesis}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The cases are most natural when read as regimes of the same marginal
reallocation idea, not as repetitions of one conclusion. FR always acts by moving
ensemble mass along the reaction coordinate, but what that buys depends on the
current bottleneck: target direction in the easy regime, variance/support in the
starved regimes, diversity control in the many-body regime, and --- in the molecular
torsions --- whether the sample starvation lives on the \emph{biased} coordinate at
all.

\subsection{Four regimes, and only one of them is mFR's}
\label{sec:three_regimes}
Taking the study as a whole, a system is characterised by \emph{three} timescales relative to
the run: how long a relevant state takes to be \textbf{found} ($T_{\mathrm{hit}}$), how long
the orthogonal degrees of freedom take to \textbf{equilibrate} at fixed reaction coordinate
($\tau_\perp$), and how long the state then takes to be \textbf{populated}
($T_{\mathrm{est}}$). The middle one was missing from the first version of this map, and
adding it costs a regime.

The reason it cannot be omitted is \cref{prop:sel_invariance}: marginal selection leaves
every conditional exactly invariant, so if the binding difficulty lives in the conditional,
the method is structurally unable to address it. Worse, if $\tau_\perp$ is long compared with
the run then the ABF mean force is itself an average over a non-equilibrium conditional
ensemble --- so the \emph{baseline} is wrong, the bias built from it is wrong, and the
occupancy targets used to test for an establishment deficit are wrong too. A discovery or
establishment verdict computed on top of an invalid baseline is not a weak verdict; it is not
a verdict. The map therefore leads with a validity gate and classifies each system by the
\textbf{first} gate it fails.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Conditional-equilibration-limited} --- the orthogonal ensemble has not
  equilibrated, $\tau_\perp \gtrsim T_{\mathrm{run}}$. Deca-alanine, and the $\beta=2$ cells
  of the $R_{15}$ study. Diagnosed \emph{before} any selection arm is run, by preparing
  independent orthogonal ensembles at fixed reaction coordinate and asking whether they agree
  on the local mean force: deca-alanine disagrees by $\GzDeca$ of $|F'|$ and the $R_{15}$
  $\beta=2$ cells by $\GzRfifteenLo$--$\GzRfifteenHi$, against $\GzWca$ for the WCA dimer and
  $\GzGateway$ for the gateway. No marginal selection rule can help here, by
  \cref{prop:sel_invariance}, and no discovery or establishment classification is admissible
  either. Deca-alanine is the cautionary case: its discovery and establishment gates both
  \emph{pass}, so on the three-regime map it would have been filed as establishment-limited
  and counted as a target for the method --- a verdict the validity gate voids.
  \item \textbf{ABF-sufficient} --- baseline valid; found early, populated early. Butane and
  pentane's $\varphi_1$, the two-dimensional torus CV, and both dipeptides of
  \cref{sec:neutrality}. The slow coordinate is inside the collective variable, ABF flattens
  it, no support deficit ever forms, and mFR is \emph{practically equivalent} to ABF in every
  matched-seed comparison. This is the largest group, and it is why the study reports far more
  nulls than positives.
  \item \textbf{Discovery-limited} --- baseline valid; never found. Pentane's $R_{15}$
  end-to-end distance at $\beta=1$ (\cref{sec:cv_harder_1d}), the only genuinely starved
  coordinate in the study. mFR cannot act: reallocation amplifies walkers that exist and
  cannot create one in a state nothing has reached. Pushed harder it converts a support
  deficit into a \emph{diversity} deficit and makes the estimate worse.
  \item \textbf{Establishment-limited} --- baseline valid; found early, populated slowly. The
  constructed entropic gateway (\cref{sec:gateway}) and the condensed-phase WCA dimer
  (\cref{sec:case_wca}). Here mFR helps, and in both systems it beats a matched sham. Both
  systems were re-examined against the validity gate after the fact and both pass, so the two
  positives in this report do not rest on an unchecked baseline.
\end{itemize}

The operative condition for the method to have anything to do is therefore not
``$T_{\mathrm{hit}}$ small'' but the ordering $T_{\mathrm{hit}} \ll \tau_\perp \ll
T_{\mathrm{est}}$: the state must be reached, its orthogonal ensemble must relax faster than
the run, and its population must nonetheless lag. That is a narrow window, which is
consistent with how rarely it arose here --- it had to be constructed on purpose
(\cref{sec:gateway}) and was found unlooked-for in exactly one physical system.

\Cref{tab:regime_map} states this benchmark by benchmark, and \cref{fig:regime_map} places
every system on the same two axes. The column order there is deliberate: the discovery and
establishment evidence are recorded \emph{before} the mFR column and are derived from
ABF-only diagnostics --- first-touch and establishment times, support fractions, occupancy
against a bias-aware target --- so the regime is never defined by the outcome it is used to
explain. Defining ``ABF-sufficient'' as ``mFR did not improve'' would make the map circular
and unfalsifiable; defining it by whether ABF has already resolved the coordinate under the
budget does not.

\Cref{sec:gw_twostate} gives the reason the last three are regimes and not a continuum. Coarse-%
grained to two states, discovery is a first-passage event among $N$ walkers and so takes
$T_{\mathrm{hit}}\sim 1/(N k_{01})$, whereas establishment is a relaxation at rate
$k_{01}+k_{10}$ and carries \emph{no factor of $N$}. Adding walkers buys discovery and not
establishment, so the window between them widens with $N$: the establishment-limited regime is
what a many-walker sampler generically produces once the crossing rate is small. Reallocation
adds a relaxation term of rate $\gamma$ that is not tied to $k_{01}$ --- which is why it helps
there, does nothing where relaxation had already finished, and vanishes identically when
$p(t)=0$ because the term is proportional to $p$.

The value of the gateway construction is that it makes the third regime reachable on demand
instead of by luck, and supplies the control the other cases lack. Because its free energy is
analytic, its map is complete, and its anchor was frozen before any FR arm ran, it can carry
the comparison that decides whether the mechanism is what it claims: a \textbf{matched sham}
that performs the same number of clone and delete events at the same times, with the direction
randomised. On $\GwcSeeds$ fresh seeds with the rates frozen in advance, mFR beats
\emph{its own sham} directly by $\GwrDirect\%$ $[\GwrDirectCIlo, \GwrDirectCIhi]$ on
$\GwrDirectWins$ seeds in the quoted replicate, and by $\GwcDirect\%$
$[\GwcDirectCIlo, \GwcDirectCIhi]$ on $\GwcDirectWins$ in the first pass
(\cref{tab:gw_anchor,tab:closure_inventory}). An estimator-independent frozen-bias endpoint
agrees in both. The separate \emph{equivalence} test on the sham does not replicate --- it
passes in the first pass and misses the $\pm\GwcMargin\%$ margin in the second
(\cref{rem:gw_replicate}) --- so the attribution here rests on the direct contrast rather than
on equivalence. Whatever mFR is doing in this regime, the majority of it is not the turnover.

\begin{remark}[The qualifier on WCA is now removed]
\label{rem:wca_possibly}
The WCA gain had only ever been compared against ABF and against other \emph{score-driven}
arms, all of which share the same replacement direction. That comparison has now been made
(\cref{sec:wca_sham}): at the accepted cell, on \WcsSeeds{} fresh seeds with nothing retuned,
practical mFR beats \emph{its own matched sham} by \WcsDirect\%
$[\WcsDirectCIlo, \WcsDirectCIhi]$ on \WcsDirectWins{} seeds. The directional mechanism
therefore transfers to a many-body molecular system, and the regime table above states it
without qualification.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The direction is attributable, but it is not the Fisher--Rao direction}
\label{sec:q1_synthesis}
The sham controls establish that the gain comes from \emph{directed} selection rather than
from turnover. They do not establish that it comes from \emph{this} direction, because until
\cref{sec:wca_q1} every comparator either was ABF or shared the Fisher--Rao score. Run against
the two directed rules already in the literature, at matched turnover and with nothing
retuned, mFR ties one of them and beats the other only because the other does not work.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Against count balancing, $S\propto 1-\hat p/\bar p$: \textbf{tied}, $\QoneCountGain\%$
  with a TOST interval $[\QoneCountTostLo, \QoneCountTostHi]$ strictly inside
  $\pm\QoneTostMargin\%$. That rule reaches $\QoneCountVsAbf\%$ against ABF on its own
  --- the whole effect.
  \item Against the Chapter~6 rule $S\propto\partial_z^2 p/p$: mFR wins by $\QoneBookGain\%$,
  but that rule is worth $\QoneBookVsAbf\%$ against ABF, i.e.\ nothing. Beating an inert
  comparator is not evidence of beating prior art.
\end{itemize}

\Cref{prop:sel_invariance} explains why a tie was the likely outcome. Every rule here is a
function of the collective variable alone, so all of them leave conditionals untouched and
differ only in the marginal they steer toward. Two rules whose targets are close --- mFR's
bias-aware target and count balancing's flat one --- have little room to separate on an
endpoint that integrates the free-energy error.

The consequence for how this work should be described is direct, and it is a
\emph{narrowing}. What survives is the diagnostic content: a map that says when selection can
help at all, a validity gate that says when the question is even well posed, a constructed
regime where the effect is real and attributable to direction rather than turnover, and a
theorem that bounds what any such rule can do. What does not survive is a performance claim
over prior art. On the one system where a positive was found unlooked-for, a one-line rule
that has been shipping in multiple-walker codes for years does just as well.

\begin{remark}[Three ways of saying ``ABF is starved'', only one of which mFR repairs]
\label{rem:starvation_kinds}
Much of the confusion this study had to work through comes from treating ``starvation'' as
one thing. The alkane and WCA analyses distinguish a \emph{support} deficit from a
\emph{dynamical} bottleneck; the dipeptide screens add a third distinction that neither
captured, between a state that is \emph{under-populated} and one that is merely
\emph{rare}. Valine's rarest region carries an unbiased population of $\ValRarePop$ and is
nonetheless established to $\ValRareOccTarget$ of its bias-aware target: rare is not starved.
Measuring against the \emph{unbiased} population rather than the bias-aware target of
\cref{eq:biasaware} would have called it starved, and would have manufactured exactly the
signal mFR is supposed to remove from a run in which nothing was wrong.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The molecular refinement: starvation must be on the biased coordinate}
The alkanes (\cref{sec:case_butane,sec:case_pentane}) sharpen the WCA ``FR helps in
proportion to ABF starvation'' finding into a precise statement:
FR reallocates mass along the \emph{biased} coordinate $\varphi_1$, so it can only
help when ABF is starved \emph{there}. In butane, ABF is not starved --- the single
dihedral mixes freely under the bias --- so mFR turns itself off and adds nothing
(within the pre-registered $\pm10\%$ margin). In pentane the visible $\varphi_1$
marginal is likewise easy to flatten, but the true difficulty is the hidden
conditional $p(\varphi_2\mid\varphi_1)$, a coordinate FR does not bias; whether
cloning whole configurations (which carry their $\varphi_2$ state) helps or harms
that conditional is the crux, and is reported from the matched-seed data rather than
assumed. \Cref{fig_alk_12_cross_case} places butane and pentane on the same
ABF-error-vs-mFR-gain map as the WCA cells, testing whether the molecular points
obey the same starvation relationship.

The collective-variable extension (\cref{sec:cv_harder_1d,sec:cv_2d,sec:cv_does_it_help})
pushes this refinement to its natural conclusion by changing the coordinate rather than the
system. Making the biased coordinate genuinely harder --- the curved end-to-end distance
$R_{15}$, which every torsional state must move through --- does produce the missing
ingredient: at $\beta=2$ ABF is, for the first time in this study, sample-starved
\emph{on the biased coordinate itself}. Yet mFR still buys nothing there (equivalent at the
deployable rate, sharply harmful when pushed). The reason completes the statement: starvation
on the biased coordinate is \emph{necessary but not sufficient}. Reallocation helps only when
the deficit is one of \emph{support} --- regions the sampler can reach but under-weights ---
and $R_{15}$ starvation is instead a \emph{dynamical} bottleneck, the rare crossing of cold
torsional barriers, which cloning can amplify but never create. Conversely, promoting the
hidden torsion into a true two-dimensional CV $(\varphi_1,\varphi_2)$ removes the starvation
altogether, because biasing both dihedrals resolves both barriers directly. Every molecular
coordinate we tried therefore fails one of the two preconditions --- either there is no
starvation on the biased coordinate ($\varphi_1$, and the two-dimensional torus CV), or the
starvation is dynamical rather than a support deficit ($R_{15}$) --- and the matched-seed
comparisons in \cref{sec:cv_harder_1d,sec:cv_does_it_help} report the resulting verdict for
each.

\subsection{A regime spectrum}
At the easy end is the metastability model (\cref{sec:case_meta}), where ABF
already converges well enough that the remaining headroom is partly about the
\emph{direction} of the target marginal. In the middle is the entropic bottleneck
(\cref{sec:case_eb}), whose temperature sweep moves the same model from easy to
starved and shows that density correction need not align with oracle-shaped free
energy steering. At the hard end is the condensed-phase WCA dimer
(\cref{sec:case_wca}), where the dimer must displace a dense solvent and the
benefit is limited by the diversity of cloned many-body configurations.

\subsection{The gain follows starvation, but the mechanism changes}
\begin{remark}[Two sign conventions, and which one is in force where]
\label{rem:sign_convention}
This paragraph is written in the \emph{gain} convention inherited from the earliest cases:
$+x\%$ means the error fell by $x\%$, so positive is better. Every closure table
(\cref{tab:synthesis,tab:closure_inventory,tab:regime_map}) and every preregistered
comparison instead reports the \emph{relative change} in error, where negative is better.
The two differ only by a sign, but they differ on the quantity a reader is most likely to
quote, so the inventory normalises all of them:
\texttt{results/closure/v1\_results\_inventory.csv} carries one column, one convention, and
the endpoint named per row.
\end{remark}

The practical, estimated-target FR gain tracks how starved the ABF estimator is.
Where ABF is a strong baseline the gain is small and not unconditional ---
$+\EstIntegImpPct\%$ on the integrated error in the metastability case, with a
marginal final-budget gain --- and where ABF is even less stressed (the hot
entropic bottleneck, $\beta\le4$) FR is mildly \emph{harmful}
($\EBwarmGainBetaFour\%$ at $\beta=4$), because birth--death injects resampling
noise into an estimator that was not starved. Where ABF is starved the gain is
large and reliable: $+\EBgainStrong\%$ in the cold bottleneck ($\EBcoldWin/20$
seeds) and $+\WCAtunedGainPct\%$ on the tuned WCA dimer ($\WCAtunedWin/10$ seeds).
In WCA the relative gain grows with simulation budget, while the densest solvent
has the largest absolute error reduction. The entropic-bottleneck temperature
sweep is the cleanest evidence because it moves along the axis \emph{within a
single system}: the same model and code flip sign from $\beta=4$ to $\beta=8$ as
ABF crosses from well-sampled to starved.

\subsection{The oracle diagnostic is regime-dependent}
The cases appear to disagree about the FR \emph{target}. In the easy metastability
case the oracle target is best and the estimated--oracle gap can be read, within
that regime, as a target-quality bottleneck (\cref{sec:q1}). In the WCA dimer the
oracle is no better than the estimated or uniform target, and in the entropic
bottleneck the oracle is actually \emph{worse than plain ABF}. This is not a
contradiction; it is the point of the comparison.

When ABF is well sampled, the remaining error can be sensitive to the target
\emph{direction}, so knowing the reference shape helps. When ABF is starved, the
first-order need is not an exact free-energy-shaped target but enough useful
support across the reaction coordinate. A rough imbalance direction can then be
sufficient, which explains why the uniform target nearly matches the estimated one
in the two starved cases. A fixed oracle target can even fight the transient ABF
bias in the cold bottleneck, so it loses to a neutral uniform target. The oracle is
therefore a diagnostic of regime, not a performance upper bound.

The oracle has a second, more constructive use: it diagnoses \emph{where} an exact
free-energy shape would pay off, even when no practical estimate captures it. The
entropy-dominant stress test (\cref{sec:eb_entropy_dominant}) makes this vivid. At
fixed total barrier, increasing the entropic share flips the cold-bottleneck
oracle from harmful to strongly beneficial (up to $+\EDBoraclePeak\%$), rising
monotonically with the entropic fraction (slope $\EDBoracleSlope\%$ per unit
$\phi$), while the deployable estimated target captures essentially none of it
(slope $\EDBestSlope\%$). The achievable benefit is thus genuinely
entropic-specific, but gated by the quality of the online free-energy target ---
the clearest single pointer this study gives for where a better estimated target
would matter.

\subsection{The shared failure mode is diversity collapse}
Both the safe regimes and the observed failure are explained by ancestor
diversity. Aggressive birth--death erodes the effective number of lineages; the
gain survives only while the orthogonal degrees of freedom re-equilibrate fast
enough to make cloned configurations useful. The WCA dimer reaches the failure
boundary (\texttt{fr\_rate}$\ge1$ reverses the benefit in the rate ladder, and even
\texttt{fr\_rate}$=0.5$ becomes harmful at production budget, ancestor ESS
$\to\!20$--$22$) because its dense solvent re-equilibrates slowly. The entropic
bottleneck does \emph{not} reach a failure boundary even at $\gamma=50$ (ESS
$\to\EBessLo$) because its analytic $y$-channel re-equilibrates almost instantly.
Same marginal-reallocation principle, different orthogonal relaxation times: a
safe FR rate must be calibrated to the system, not copied across examples.
