\section{Harder one-dimensional molecular coordinates: end-to-end distance}
\label{sec:cv_harder_1d}

The $\varphi_1$ studies (\cref{sec:case_butane,sec:case_pentane}) left one mechanism
untested: in both alkanes ABF resolved the biased coordinate \emph{easily}, so marginal
Fisher--Rao (mFR) never had a support deficit to repair. To probe whether mFR helps when
ABF is genuinely sample-starved \emph{along the biased coordinate}, we replace the torsion
by a harder but chemically meaningful scalar: the pentane end-to-end distance
\begin{equation}
  \xi(q) = R_{15}(q) = \lvert q_5 - q_1\rvert ,
\end{equation}
which measures molecular extension/compactness and depends jointly on both torsions and on
the bond/angle fluctuations. Many torsional states map to the same $R_{15}$, so the hidden
conditional here is the full joint torsion law $p(\varphi_1,\varphi_2\mid R_{15})$. As a
secondary, easier control we also study the butane $R_{14}=\lvert q_4-q_1\rvert$.

\subsection{Distance CV, generalized mean force, and reference}
With $r=q_5-q_1$, $R=\lvert r\rvert$, $e=r/R$ we have $\nabla_{q_5}R=e$, $\nabla_{q_1}R=-e$,
$\lvert\nabla R\rvert^2=2$, and (because $H_R\nabla R=0$) the den~Otter geometric term is
exactly $\nabla\!\cdot v = 2/R$ for $v=\nabla R/\lvert\nabla R\rvert^2$, giving the local
mean force
\begin{equation}
  f_R(q) = \tfrac12\,e\cdot\!\big(\nabla_{q_5}V-\nabla_{q_1}V\big) - \frac{2}{\beta R}.
\end{equation}
The analytic gradient/divergence match autodiff to $<10^{-9}/10^{-7}$; $R$ never approaches
$0$ under the stiff bonds, and identical soft walls confine every method to a reliable
interval. Because $R_{15}$ is non-periodic we use reflected-boundary KDE and a
left-anchored trapezoidal PMF. The reference is built by
internal-coordinate importance sampling: bonds/angles from their exact Boltzmann+Jacobian
law, dihedrals from $e^{-\beta V_4}$, reweighted by $e^{-\beta V_\mathrm{nb}}$ (the 1--5 LJ;
butane has none, so its reference is exact by construction). The primary (`$V_4$-proposal')
estimator has effective-sample fraction \CvRfifteenEss; an independent uniform-proposal scheme
agrees to \CvRfifteenCross~kT in the thermal window (range \CvRfifteenRange~kT), well inside the
15\% success threshold. A minimal resolution gate ($n_\mathrm{grid}\in\{128,256\}$,
bandwidth $\{h,h/2\}$) leaves the reference shape and the ABF diagnosis unchanged.

\subsection{ABF-only starvation screen: R15 is the first genuinely starved coordinate}
We ran ABF alone across $\beta\in\{1,2\}$ (butane $R_{14}$: $\beta\in\{1,2,3\}$) with
localized-trans and dispersed initializations, classifying each cell by \emph{distinct
evidence families} of the pre-registered criteria: a magnitude/plateau family
(normalized residual $L_2$ above the estimator floor), a support family
(under-supported thermal $R$ bins), a mixing family (compact$\leftrightarrow$extended
round trips per replica), and a conditional family (biased $p(\varphi_1,\varphi_2\mid R)$).
Because the raw magnitude and plateau signals are not statistically independent, we count
them as one family and require $\geq 2$ \emph{distinct} families for a ``starved'' verdict.
\Cref{tab:cv_r15_screen} reports the outcome.

Unlike the $\varphi_1$ torsion --- which ABF resolves easily --- the pentane $R_{15}$
coordinate is classified \textbf{\CvRfifteenStarv} at $\beta=2$ (thermal $L_2\approx14$--16\%
of the free-energy range, $\sim$2.5$\times$ the estimator floor, with $\sim$22\% of thermal
$R$ bins under-supported and, in the dispersed cell, near-absent compact$\leftrightarrow$
extended mixing). It is easy at $\beta=1$. The butane control $R_{14}$ is \textbf{easy} at
$\beta\in\{1,2\}$ and only \textbf{\CvRfourteenStarv} at $\beta=3$. This yields a clean,
graded picture: a chemically meaningful \emph{distance} CV \emph{can} make ABF sample-starved
along the biased coordinate once the torsional barriers that change the distance are rarely
crossed, and the difficulty is ordered by molecule --- pentane $R_{15}$ reaches a full
\emph{starved} verdict already at $\beta=2$, whereas the easier butane $R_{14}$ never does at
any temperature tested, reaching only \emph{intermediate} (a single evidence family: high
error, but no support or mixing deficit) at the colder $\beta=3$. A minimal resolution gate leaves the
$R_{15}$ starvation diagnosis unchanged ($L_2$ 21--22\% across $n_\mathrm{grid}\in\{128,256\}$
and bandwidth $\{h,h/2\}$). A run-length control settles the obvious alternative explanation
--- that the cell is merely under-converged at a fixed budget rather than starved: doubling
the ABF budget from $80$k to $160$k steps reduces the thermal $L_2$ only from $15.1\%$ to
$13.7\%$ of the range (a $9\%$ relative gain for $2\times$ the compute), leaves the error
\emph{plateaued} (final-quarter change $-0.04$, i.e.\ no longer decreasing) still far above the
$\sim$6--7\% estimator floor, and does \emph{not} fill the under-supported bins (low-support
fraction $0.22\to0.28$). The deficit is therefore a genuine \emph{support} starvation that more
of the same sampling does not cure.
Because a starved cell exists, the pre-registered gate triggers the full $R_{15}$ mFR/OPES
comparison below --- the study's one clean opportunity for mFR to help.

\subsection{Does mFR rescue a starved ABF? No.}
On the starved $R_{15}$ ($\beta=2$, localized trans) cell we ran the full ladder at matched
seeds, initial conditions, replicas, steps and force budget. The result is unambiguous and,
notably, \emph{the same as when ABF is not starved}: mFR gives \textbf{no improvement}. The
deployable estimated-target mFR (rate $0.02$) is \textbf{\CvRfifteenmFRVerdict} to ABF
(median change in final $L_2$: \CvRfifteenmFRRelFinal\,\%; win rate \CvRfifteenmFRWin). At
production scale (eight matched seeds) ABF reaches a median final $L_2$ of $1.500$ and the
deployable mFR $1.532$ ($+2.1\%$, inside the $\pm10\%$ practical-equivalence margin) with the
hidden-conditional fidelity unchanged ($0.072$ both) --- and mFR fails to beat ABF on
\emph{every one} of the eight matched seeds (win rate $\CvRfifteenmFRWin$). The labelled
aggressive probe (rate $0.10$) is severely \emph{harmful}: $2.015$ ($+34.4\%$), with the
ancestor ESS collapsing to $0.06\,N$ and the conditional degrading to $0.087$.

Two diagnostics make the negative result decisive rather than a tuning artifact. First, the
\emph{uniform} and \emph{oracle} targets perform identically to the estimated one
($1.533$, $+2.2\%$ each): even a target built from the \emph{exact reference free energy} ---
the quantity being computed --- buys nothing. The failure is therefore not poor target
estimation but the reallocation mechanism itself. Second, the OPES baseline was swept
independently on held-out seeds for this cell over barrier $\in\{8,16,24\}$, kernel width
$\sigma\in\{0.05,0.10\}$ and deposition pace $\in\{200,400\}$: its \emph{best} configuration
(barrier $8$, $\sigma=0.05$, pace $200$) still trails ABF by $+88\%$, larger barriers are
monotonically worse ($+153$ to $+156\%$), and --- most tellingly --- \emph{every} configuration
records \textbf{zero} compact$\leftrightarrow$extended round trips (the production
configuration also degrades the conditional to $0.192$). An OPES bias grows only where walkers
have already been, so on this steep cold profile no setting mobilises the barrier crossing at
all. The same bottleneck therefore defeats a
different marginal-biasing strategy, which is evidence about the \emph{coordinate}, not about
any one algorithm. The rate ladder is correspondingly
\emph{monotone in the wrong direction}: final $L_2$ rises from the ABF value as the mFR rate
increases ($+0.4,+1.0,+4.4,+13.4$\% at rates $0.005,0.02,0.05,0.10$) while the ancestor ESS
falls ($0.97\!\to\!0.87\!\to\!0.62\!\to\!0.17$ of $N$). There is no useful
window. \Cref{fig_cv_r15_methods} shows the paired comparison, the rate ladder, and the
hidden-conditional fidelity.

\paragraph{Provenance of the deployed rate.} We record this precisely, because the ladder is
monotone and therefore makes the selection rule degenerate. The pre-registered rule --- the
smallest median integrated $L_2(F)$ on the held-out tuning seeds subject to the genealogy
guard --- has its argmin at the \emph{gentlest} rung, $0.005$ (median integrated
$L_2=83.30$ against ABF's $83.16$), not at the $0.02$ we deploy ($83.58$); the two stiffer
rungs, $0.05$ and $0.10$, are worse still ($84.50$ and $85.94$), and only $0.10$ fails the
genealogy guard. In other words the rule, applied literally to a monotone ladder, selects the rate that
does the \emph{least}, because doing less is what minimises the error. We deploy $0.02$
instead --- the gentlest rung at which birth--death is meaningfully active (ancestor ESS
$0.87\,N$ rather than $0.97\,N$) --- so that the reported comparison actually exercises the
mechanism under test. This choice is \emph{conservative against} mFR, not for it: the rule's
own pick would have scored slightly better ($+0.3\%$ versus $+1.3\%$ on the tuning seeds).
Every genealogy-passing rung is classified \emph{equivalent} to ABF, so the verdict does not
depend on which is quoted; what the degeneracy shows is that on this coordinate the best
available mFR configuration is the one closest to not running mFR at all.

The mechanism is diagnostic (\cref{sec:cv_does_it_help}, Q3): $R_{15}$ starvation is a
\emph{dynamical} bottleneck --- extended replicas rarely cross the cold torsional barriers
needed to reach compact $R$ --- not a \emph{support} deficit that marginal reallocation can
fill. The support diagnostics locate the failure precisely, and the answer is more
interesting than ``reallocation cannot fill the gap''. At the deployable rate mFR barely fires
(ancestor ESS $0.79\,N$), so the support profile is essentially untouched (under-supported
fraction $0.218$ versus ABF's $0.223$) and the error with it. Pushed to rate $0.10$ it does
exactly what it is designed to do --- the under-supported fraction falls by nearly two thirds,
$0.223\to0.082$ --- and yet the free energy becomes markedly \emph{worse} ($+34\%$). The reason
is that the newly ``supported'' bins are filled with \emph{clones} rather than independent
samples: ancestor ESS collapses to $0.06\,N$ and the round-trip count per replica actually
\emph{falls} ($8.9\to6.0$), because independently evolving replicas are replaced by copies of
the few that had already crossed. Reallocation thus converts a support deficit into a
\emph{diversity} deficit: nominal occupancy improves while the effective sample size --- the
quantity the mean-force estimator actually consumes --- degrades. Amplification is not
discovery. The independently tuned OPES baseline and
the diagnostic uniform/oracle targets are reported alongside in \cref{tab:cv_r15_methods}.

The frozen-bias fairness control (freeze $B(R)$, run fresh dynamics with no updates and no
birth--death, reconstruct $F=B-\beta^{-1}\log p_B+C$) delivers a second, independent
confirmation that this cell is genuinely starved --- and it behaves \emph{oppositely} to the
converged $\varphi_1$ case. There, freezing the learned bias reconstructed $F$ far more
accurately than the online mean-force integral ($0.05$ vs $0.27$), proving the online residual
was a mere estimator-resolution floor. Here the reconstruction is \emph{worse} than the online
estimate for both methods (ABF $1.90$ vs $1.48$; mFR $1.92$ vs $1.51$, seed-averaged
profiles): the learned $B(R)$ is itself inaccurate because the underlying sampling is
deficient, and freezing it does not help since the frozen dynamics remain trapped by the same
rare-crossing bottleneck. The residual is therefore \emph{not} a resolution artifact but real
under-sampling --- exactly the distinction the starvation screen is meant to detect --- and
mFR's learned bias is no better than ABF's, consistent with their practical equivalence.
