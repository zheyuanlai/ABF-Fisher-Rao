\section{The validity layer: what had to be true before any classification could be read}
\label{sec:validity}

Every case section above assigns its system to a regime, and every such assignment presupposes
that the ABF baseline it was measured against is itself sound. That presupposition failed once,
in deca-alanine (\cref{sec:deca}), and the failure was not visible in any convergence diagnostic
the study was running at the time. This section collects the machinery built in response: the
definition of Gate 0, the retrospective application of it to systems classified before it
existed, the correction of a reference that was wrong by tens of standard deviations, and a
cross-system audit asking whether any deployable indicator predicts when marginal reallocation
helps. The answer to that last question is no, and it constrains what the rest of the report can
claim.

\subsection{Gate 0, and the clause that was retracted}
\label{sec:gate0}

Gate 0 asks, from ABF-only data plus the accepted reference, whether the conditional mean force
can be trusted at fixed $\xi$. It was introduced as Amendment~7 and moved to the head of the gate
order by Amendment~10, so classification proceeds
\[
  \text{Gate 0} \;\to\; \text{A} \;\to\; \text{B} \;\to\; \text{C},
\]
with the regime assigned by the \emph{first} failing gate. Failing Gate 0 classifies a system as
\emph{ABF-baseline-invalid}, and the later gates are not evaluated at all --- not merely
downweighted, but not computed, because their inputs are built from the object under suspicion.

The gate as originally written had two clauses. The first required the learned span to fall
within $[0.75, 1.25]$ of the reference span; it was \textbf{retracted} by Amendment~9 after the
$R_{15}$ guard audit showed it firing on all four $R_{15}$ cells at
$\VrSpanClauseLo$--$\VrSpanClauseHi$, including the two $\beta = 1$ cells that every other
diagnostic calls easy. The conclusion recorded at the time was that \emph{the clause is wrong,
not $R_{15}$}, and it was deleted rather than re-tuned. The second clause, retained, requires that
no seed hold more than $0.90$ of its walkers in one tercile across the whole second half; it
discriminates cleanly, separating deca-alanine's $\DcPinLo$--$\DcPinHi$ from $R_{15}$'s
$\VrPinLo$--$\VrPinHi$.

The statistic the gate actually reports --- the relative disagreement in the local mean force
across independently prepared orthogonal pools held at the same $\xi$ --- deliberately carries
\textbf{no numerical threshold}. Setting one at the time would have fixed $R_{15}$'s verdict
after seeing $R_{15}$'s number. The gate is read against a calibration ladder instead:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcl}
\toprule
system & spread / $\overline{|F'|}$ & reading \\
\midrule
NaCl (\cref{sec:nacl})            & $\NaGateZeroGlobal$   & pass, campaign best \\
gateway (\cref{sec:gateway})      & $\GzGateway$          & pass globally, $\GzGatewayLocal$ in the constriction \\
WCA (\cref{sec:case_wca})              & $\GzWca$              & pass, $\GzWcaTransition$ through the transition \\
methane (\cref{sec:methane})      & $\MeGateZeroGlobal$   & pass \\
$R_{15}$, $\beta=2$ (\cref{sec:cv_harder_1d}) & $\GzRfifteenLo$--$\GzRfifteenHi$ & fails locally \\
deca-alanine (\cref{sec:deca})    & $\DcGateZero$         & \textbf{fails} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The ladder is the instrument, and the controlled orthogonal-pool experiment is what it is made
of; the gate is a screen for when to run that experiment, not a substitute for it.

\subsection{Backfilling the systems classified before the gate existed}

WCA and the entropic gateway were classified establishment-limited --- and the gateway's positive
result obtained --- before Gate 0 was written. Both classifications were therefore
\emph{provisional} until the gate was applied retrospectively, and this section reports that
backfill rather than assuming it.

For WCA the design is $\VgWcaZvals$ values of $z$ $\times$ $\VgWcaPools$ independently prepared
pools --- a lattice start, starts driven from below and from above the transition, and a
randomised hot start --- $\times$ $\VgWcaReplicas$ replicas, with $\VgWcaPrepSteps$ steps of
preparation and equilibration each before production. The pools agree to $\GzWca$ globally and
$\GzWcaTransition$ through the transition region, so the establishment-limited classification and
the Case IX positive resting on it survive. The backfill does \emph{not} validate the magnitude
of that positive, and it was in the course of running it that the second and larger problem
surfaced.

\begin{remark}[The pools agreed with each other and disagreed with the reference]
\label{rem:gate0_two_readings}
At $z = \VgWcaWorstZ$ the four pools agreed among themselves while sitting $\VgWcaWorstGap\,\%$
away from the cached reference. A statistic normalised by $|F'_{\rm ref}|$ cannot distinguish
``the pools disagree'' from ``the reference is wrong'', and the first reading is the one the gate
is written to detect. Against the corrected reference the same comparison falls from
$\VgWcaPoolVsRefOld$ to $\VgWcaPoolVsRefNew$ --- the pools had been right all along. Gate 0's
normalised figures are therefore \emph{not} reference-independent, and a large value must be
resolved into its two possible causes before it is read as a conditional-equilibration failure.
\end{remark}

For the gateway the global figure is $\GzGateway$ and it passes, but the local figure is the one
that matters and it is the weakest pass in the campaign. Inside the constriction --- the region
where the mFR effect is generated --- the pools disagree by $\GzGatewayLocal$, and the
second-moment version of the same statistic rises from $\VgGwYtwoAll$ to $\VgGwYtwoLocal$. The
timescale separation $T_{\rm hit} \ll \tau_\perp \ll T_{\rm est}$ that the regime definition
assumes is also not literally satisfied there: median residence in the constriction is
$\VgGwResidence$ against $\tau_\perp = \VgGwTauPerp$, a ratio of only $\VgGwResidenceRatio$, and
the residence distribution is heavy-tailed with a mean $\VgGwTailRatio\times$ its median over
$\VgGwCrossings$ crossings. We state this plainly because it cuts against our own positive
result: \emph{the gateway's mFR effect lives exactly where its conditional ensemble is least
well equilibrated.} Two mitigating facts, neither decisive. The audit used a simplified ABF
reimplementation rather than the accepted sampler, so it is a conservative instrument. And the
gateway's attribution rests on the direct arm-versus-sham contrast (\cref{sec:gw_confirm}),
in which both arms experience the same conditional, so a shared conditional error does not
manufacture the difference between them --- though it could still distort its magnitude.

\subsection{A reference that was wrong by sixty-seven standard deviations}
\label{sec:reference_correction}

The WCA free-energy reference used through v1 carried a spurious feature near $z = \VtTroughZ$. A
dense rescan at spacing $\VtDenseDz$ found $F'$ there to be $\VtTroughNew$ against the cached
$\VtTroughCached$, a deviation of $\VtTroughSigma$ standard deviations, with ten consecutive
points tracing a resolved descent and recovery and per-preparation values spanning only $0.171$
--- so it is a real feature of the physics, not a preparation artifact.

Two independent causes combined. The cached grid used a spacing of $\VtCachedDz$, too coarse to
resolve the feature at all. And the smoothing routine took its bandwidth in \emph{grid cells}
rather than in physical units of the collective variable, which converted a raw value of
$\VtSmoothRaw$ into a smoothed $\VtSmoothed$. Neither is a modelling choice; both are unit
errors in analysis code that ran without complaint.

The corrected high-precision reference is built on $\VtRefStates$ states --- $41$ values of $z$,
four preparations, $128$ replicas --- in $\VtRefMinutes$ minutes, and puts $F'$ at $z = 0.25$ at
$\VtRefNew$ against the cached $\VtRefCached$ with a between-preparation standard error of
$\VtRefSe$, a $\VtRefSigma\sigma$ discrepancy. A third build removed interpolation from the
question entirely by acquiring on the evaluation grid itself, so that the interpolant is the
identity ($\VtVthreeInterp$ maximum difference between two interpolation schemes); it places the
feature at $z = \VtVthreeZ$ with value $\VtVthreeValue$.

\begin{remark}[Why this had to be a rescore of fresh dynamics, not of stored curves]
\label{rem:why_fresh}
The shift in the whole reference profile is $\VtRefShift$ in $L^2$. Case IX's ABF arm has a
median final error of $\VtAbfError$ and its mFR arm $\VtMfrError$, so the correction is
$\VtShiftOverAbf$ of ABF's own error and $\VtShiftOverEffect\times$ the effect size being
claimed. A reference correction larger than the effect cannot be absorbed as a caveat. The v1
runs stored only the scalar error trace and the final profile, never the time-resolved estimate,
so rescoring against the corrected reference was impossible from the archive and the confirmatory
comparison had to be re-run as fresh dynamics. \emph{The uncalibrated effect size was the
finding}: an improvement quoted to four significant figures against a reference wrong by three
times that improvement is not a measurement.
\end{remark}

The direction of the correction was itself checked rather than assumed. Rescoring the paired
comparison on a final-time endpoint under both references over $\VcSeeds$ seeds moves mFR versus
ABF from $\VcOldMfr\,\%$ to $\VcNewMfr\,\%$ (CI $[\VcNewCIlo, \VcNewCIhi]$) and the direct
arm-versus-sham contrast from $\VcOldDirect\,\%$ to $\VcNewDirect\,\%$, while the sham-versus-ABF
comparison moves from $\VcOldSham\,\%$ to $\VcNewSham\,\%$. Every sign is $\VcSigns$; the
magnitudes fall by roughly a quarter. The correction changed how large the effect is, not which
direction it points.

We also record the convention audit run alongside, since a wrong reference invites suspicion of
the geometry: in float64 the analytic $\nabla \xi$ matches autodiff to $0$, $|\nabla \xi|^2$
evaluates to $0.125$ exactly, and the divergence, den~Otter and projection terms agree to
$10^{-9}$ or better. The geometry was never in question; the grid and the smoother were.

\subsection{$R_{15}$: an inconclusive instrument, and a verdict that survived it}
\label{sec:r15_audits}

The $R_{15}$ distance coordinate of \cref{sec:cv_harder_1d} was classified discovery-limited, and three
audits tested that.

The first asks whether the minimum-count guard, set at $\VrGuard$, materially changed the
published numbers. It did not: the worst change in $\|F\|_{L^2}$ is $\VrGuardDeltaL$ and in the
span ratio $\VrGuardDeltaSpan$, and the v1 figures reproduce exactly. This is the audit that also
retracted Gate 0's span clause, as above.

The second attempted an independent restrained measurement of the conditional mean force and
returned \textbf{inconclusive} rather than a verdict. Build-to-build disagreement was
$\VrRestrainedBuild$ against its own agreement threshold of $\VrRestrainedThresh$, so the
instrument could not resolve the quantity it was built to measure. The cause is a defect in the
instrument, not in $R_{15}$: the umbrella constant of $\VrRestrainedKumb$~kJ/mol/nm$^2$ was too
soft, so a window nominally centred at $1.550$~nm sampled a mean of $1.943$~nm and the region
below $\VrUncovered$~nm was not covered at all. We report it as inconclusive rather than folding
it into either direction.

The third measured the conditional directly at six separations, and its per-run scripts printed
the wrong criterion: they reported the maximum total variation between pools, which reaches
$1.000$ at $R = 2.90$~nm where the mean-force spread is $0.017$ --- a total variation of one
between two pools that agree on the quantity of interest. Read on the mean force instead, Gate 0
fails locally in the band $[\VrBandLo, \VrBandHi]$~nm. That does not overturn the
discovery-limited classification, and the reason is a decomposition rather than a judgement call:
the failing band carries $\VrBandError\,\%$ of the total error over $\VrBandMask\,\%$ of the
scoring mask, which is sub-proportional, while $\VrTailError\,\%$ of the error sits in the
extended tail where the coordinate is starved. $R_{15}$ remains discovery-limited with a
documented local caveat.

\subsection{No deployable indicator predicts when marginal reallocation helps}
\label{sec:mechanism_audit}

The regime map is diagnostic in retrospect. The practical question is whether a practitioner,
holding only an ABF baseline run and no reference free energy, could decide in advance whether to
switch on a Fisher--Rao arm. We tested that on $\VmRuns$ runs across $\VmCells$ cells and
$\VmSystems$ systems, with the practical-equivalence margin of $\pm\VmMargin\,\%$ and the
candidate indicator list both fixed before any performance number was examined.

The protocol is leave-one-\emph{system}-out. Each candidate indicator's direction and threshold
are fitted on the training systems, the held-out system's scores are standardised using the
training statistics alone, and the pooled out-of-sample AUC is bootstrapped within system. The
unit of observation is one system--cell pair, so a system contributing many cells cannot
manufacture its own validation.

The preregistered primary gate --- the number of transverse relaxation times available per bin,
on a log scale --- achieves an out-of-sample AUC of $\VmGateAuc$ with a $95\,\%$ interval of
$[\VmGateCIlo, \VmGateCIhi]$, a balanced accuracy of $\VmGateBalAcc$, and a fitted \emph{sign}
that flips between folds. It is below chance, and its in-sample AUC of $\VmGateInSample$ shows
where the apparent signal came from. The best predictor overall is the measured ABF error itself
at $\VmIncAAuc$ $[\VmIncACIlo, \VmIncACIhi]$ with a stable sign --- but that quantity requires the
reference free energy, which is what the method exists to estimate, so it is a retrospective
diagnostic and not a deployable gate. The best genuinely deployable feature, a support-uniformity
ratio, reads $\VmSupportAuc$ $[\VmSupportCIlo, \VmSupportCIhi]$; dropping the one system whose
generating code was never committed collapses it to $\VmSupportNoOrphan$, while the
non-deployable incumbent only falls to $\VmIncANoOrphan$. Three of the indicators we most wanted
--- round-trip fractions, median first-passage time over the horizon, and the number of distinct
crossing lineages --- are recorded as \textbf{unavailable in every system}, because the engines
never persisted the counters they need.

We state the conclusion at the strength the artifacts support, and no further: on this evidence
\emph{no deployable ABF-only indicator predicts the sign of the mFR gain out of sample}. The
$\rho = +0.80$ correlation between ABF error and mFR gain reported for WCA in
\cref{sec:wca_starvation} is not contradicted --- ABF error is the one thing that does predict ---
but it is not usable, and \cref{sec:cv_harder_1d} already showed that extrapolating it across systems
fails outright.

Two further decompositions come from the same audit. First, the error budget is almost entirely
\emph{bias}: at the WCA representative cell the bias fraction is $\VmBiasFracAbf$ for ABF and
$\VmBiasFracMfr$ for mFR, and across all $135$ cases it never falls below $\VmBiasFracLo$. On
matched seeds mFR's variance is $\VmVarRatio\times$ ABF's, with an interval
$[\VmVarRatioCIlo, \VmVarRatioCIhi]$ that includes one, while its mean squared error is
$\VmMseRatio\times$ ABF's $[\VmMseRatioCIlo, \VmMseRatioCIhi]$ and its squared bias
$\VmBiasRatio\times$. Marginal reallocation is a bias-reduction mechanism that costs a little
variance; describing it as variance reduction, as we did in an earlier draft, was wrong. The
decomposition's own cross-check reproduces every stored error to $\VmCrossCheck$.

Second, the shape of the Fisher--Rao target mostly cannot be resolved. Over $\VmAblCells$ cells
and $\VmAblRows$ matched seed pairs, with equivalence assessed by a $\VmAblBoot$-resample paired
bootstrap against a margin of $5\,\%$ of each cell's own ABF baseline error, $\VmAblU$ cells are
underpowered or lack an oracle arm, $\VmAblA$ show all three contrasts equivalent, $\VmAblB$ show
the estimated and uniform targets equivalent with the oracle differing, and $\VmAblC$ show all
three differing. At the level of individual contrasts the primary metric gives $\VmAblUnderpowered$
underpowered, $\VmAblDifferent$ different and $\VmAblEquivalent$ equivalent. The honest summary is
that target shape is first-order in a small identified minority of cells --- the WCA starved cells
and parts of the entropy-dominant sweep --- indistinguishable in the alkane torsions, and
\emph{not measurable} in most of the rest at the seed counts we ran.

\begin{remark}[What this audit could not do]
A split-half decomposition by ancestral family, which would have separated selection noise from
sampling noise directly, was attempted and recorded as \emph{impossible from existing artifacts}:
the engines keep ancestor labels only in memory and every system shares one global mean-force
accumulator across replicas. It needs new instrumented runs, and we did not run them. We also
note that a second, differently-scoped pass of the mixing-gate analysis exists on a divergent
branch with numbers that cannot be reconciled with the artifacts reported here; the figures above
are those of the current tree and the two sets must not be mixed.
\end{remark}
