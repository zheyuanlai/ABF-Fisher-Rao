\section{Building the missing regime: an entropic gateway}
\label{sec:gateway}

Every system in this study so far has landed in one of two regimes, and neither of them
tests marginal reallocation. The alkane torsions, the two-dimensional torus CV and the two
dipeptides of \cref{sec:neutrality} are \emph{ABF-sufficient}: the slow coordinate is in the
collective variable, ABF flattens it, and every state is populated long before the run ends,
so no deficit ever forms. The $R_{15}$ distance CV of \cref{sec:cv_harder_1d} is
\emph{discovery-limited}: the state is never reached, and reallocation cannot clone a walker
that does not exist. The regime mFR is actually supposed to serve --- a state that is
\emph{found early} but \emph{populated slowly} --- has never appeared on its own.

The response is not to try more molecules. If the slow coordinate is included in the CV,
ABF will flatten it again; if it is omitted, the system becomes discovery-limited or
conditionally non-ergodic, where marginal reallocation provably cannot act. Either way the
outcome is decided by the choice of coordinate rather than by the method, and searching for
a molecule that happens to fall between the two is example hunting. We instead construct the
missing regime deliberately, in a model where discovery and establishment can be moved
independently and the free energy is known in closed form.

\subsection{Model and screening design}
We reuse the smooth entropic channel of \cref{sec:case_eb},
\begin{equation}
  V_{s,r}(x,y) = H(x^2-1)^2 + \tfrac12\,\omega_{s,r}(x)^2 y^2,
  \qquad
  \omega_{s,r}(x) = \omega_{\mathrm{out}} + (\omega_{\mathrm{in}}-\omega_{\mathrm{out}})
                    e^{-x^2/2s^2},
  \qquad \xi(x,y) = x,
  \label{eq:gateway}
\end{equation}
with $r = \omega_{\mathrm{in}}/\omega_{\mathrm{out}} > 1$ and the analytic free energy
$F_{s,r}(x) = H(x^2-1)^2 + \beta^{-1}\log\omega_{s,r}(x) + C$. Because $F$ is exact there is
no reference simulation to converge and no reference error to confound the classification ---
the single largest advantage over any molecular alternative. The constriction at $x=0$ is a
narrow entropic gateway: a walker can occasionally cross, repeated independent crossings stay
slow, the far chamber is visible in $\xi$, and a discovered walker there is a valid sample
that reallocation could multiply. That is precisely the mechanism mFR claims.

The screen is \textbf{ABF only} --- no Fisher--Rao arm of any kind --- over
$s \in \{0.10,0.15,0.20,0.30\}$, $r \in \{4,8,16,32\}$ and
$\beta \in \{2,4,8,16\}$, at $\GwSeeds$ seeds, $N=\GwWalkers$ walkers and $T=\GwTrun$, in
both a concentrated left-basin initialisation and a control in which one walker starts in
the right basin. We hold $\beta H = 8\,k_BT$ fixed, so that
$\beta F(x) = \beta H (x^2-1)^2 + \log\omega(x)$ carries no residual $\beta$ dependence: the
\emph{dimensionless landscape is identical in every cell}, the total barrier is
$8 + \log r \in [\GwBarrierLo, \GwBarrierHi]\,k_BT$ throughout, and $\beta$ varies only how
fast walkers move through it, at an unchanged sampling budget.

\begin{remark}[The $\beta$ axis is a time budget, not a family of landscapes]
\label{rem:gw_beta}
A sizing scan established that $(s,r)$ alone does not span the regimes: at fixed $\beta$ the
gateway geometry moves the establishment gap only weakly, monotonically and in the expected
direction. Reporting a single $(s,r)$ slice would therefore have hidden the axis that does
the work --- but it is essential to be precise about what that axis \emph{is}, and it is not
a family of different physical problems.

Because $\beta H$ is held fixed, $\beta F(x)$ is identical in every cell by construction.
Substituting $\tilde y = \sqrt{\beta}\,y$ and $\tilde b = \beta b$ and rescaling time by
$\tau = t/\beta$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \dd x = -\big[G(x,\tilde y) - \tilde b(x)\big]\dd\tau + \sqrt2\,\dd W_\tau,
  \qquad
  \dd\tilde y = -\beta\,\omega^2 \tilde y \dd\tau + \sqrt{2\beta}\,\dd W_\tau,
  \label{eq:gw_rescale}
\end{equation}
with $G = 4\beta H x(x^2-1) + \omega\omega'\tilde y^2$. The \emph{longitudinal} equation ---
the one the collective variable lives on --- is exactly $\beta$-free. $\beta$ survives only
as the transverse relaxation rate relative to longitudinal motion, and in the direction that
makes large $\beta$ \emph{more} adiabatic: as $\beta\to\infty$ the fast $\tilde y$ averages,
$\langle\omega\omega'\tilde y^2\rangle = (\log\omega)'$, so
$\langle G\rangle = \dd(\beta F)/\dd x$ and the limiting dynamics has no $\beta$ at all.

The measurement agrees. Across the $\BsBetaFold\times$ ladder, $T_{\mathrm{est}}/T_{\mathrm{run}}$
varies $\BsTestFracFold\times$ --- which is what made it look like a regime axis --- while
$\tau_{\mathrm{est}}$ varies only $\BsTauEstFold\times$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{hit}}$ is identical
to four digits ($\BsTauHit$). The residual drifts downward with $\beta$, the sign predicted by
finite-$\beta$ non-adiabaticity.

\textbf{We therefore report this table as a finite-budget establishment map}, not as a phase
diagram over physically different equilibrium problems. The collapse is not exact, and what
breaks it is worth naming: the ABF estimator does \emph{not} rescale --- counts accumulate per
step, so each cell receives $\beta$ times more samples per unit $\tau$, and the time-averaged
error still varies $\BsMeanErrFold\times$ --- followed by finite-$\beta$ non-adiabaticity and
the FR event frequency per unit $\tau$. Bandwidths live in $x$ and are invariant.

None of this weakens the comparison that follows. Methods are always evaluated at finite
budget, and a regime defined by a ratio of timescales can only be reached by moving that
ratio. What it forbids is the claim that varying $\beta$ produced different free-energy
landscapes; it did not, and \cref{eq:gw_rescale} is why.
\end{remark}

With $T_{\mathrm{hit}}$ the first time $\widehat P_t(B_+) > 0$ persistently and
$T_{\mathrm{est}}$ the first time $\tfrac12 Q^\star_+(t) \le \widehat P_t(B_+) \le
\tfrac32 Q^\star_+(t)$ persistently --- both on a trailing window of $5\%$ of the run,
against the \emph{bias-aware} target
$Q^\star_+(t) = \int_{B_+} e^{-\beta(F-\widehat F_t)} \big/ \int e^{-\beta(F-\widehat F_t)}$
--- each cell is labelled by a rule fixed before any of it ran:
\emph{discovery-limited} if $T_{\mathrm{hit}} \ge 0.1\,T$ in at least a quarter of seeds;
otherwise \emph{establishment-limited} if $T_{\mathrm{est}}-T_{\mathrm{hit}} > 0.25\,T$ or
the state is below half its target for at least $0.2\,T$;
otherwise \emph{ABF-sufficient} if $T_{\mathrm{est}}-T_{\mathrm{hit}} < 0.1\,T$;
otherwise \emph{intermediate}. Discovery is tested first because a state that is not
reliably found cannot be judged on how fast it fills.

\subsection{The finite-budget establishment map}
\Cref{fig_gw_01_phase_diagram} reports all $\GwCells$ cells, including every cell where mFR
could not possibly help. Of these, $\GwSuff$ are ABF-sufficient, $\GwInter$ intermediate,
$\GwEst$ establishment-limited and $\GwDisc$ discovery-limited. The map is layered in
$\beta$: $\beta=2$ is ABF-sufficient everywhere, $\beta=4$ intermediate everywhere,
$\beta=8$ mixed, and $\beta=16$ establishment-limited everywhere. Per \cref{rem:gw_beta} that
layering is a statement about how much rescaled time a fixed step budget buys, not about
different landscapes. No cell of this model is discovery-limited; that regime is represented
in this study by $R_{15}$, not here.

\subsection{The anchor, chosen before any Fisher--Rao arm existed}
The classification was written to disk, hashed and committed \emph{before} a single
Fisher--Rao arm was run on this system, and the anchor follows from a rule stated in that
same artifact: the headline initialisation; regime establishment-limited; \emph{interior,
not knife-edge}, meaning every neighbour along $s$, $r$ and $\beta$ present in the map shares
its regime; then the largest median integrated deficit among the survivors. This selects
\begin{equation*}
  \beta = \GwAnchorBeta, \qquad s = \GwAnchorS, \qquad r = \GwAnchorR .
\end{equation*}
Under ABF alone the right chamber there is reached at $T_{\mathrm{hit}}/T = \GwAnchorThit$ by
\emph{every} seed, reaches its establishment band only at
$T_{\mathrm{est}}/T = \GwAnchorTest$, sits below half its bias-aware target for
$\GwAnchorBelowHalf$ of the run, and ends at $\GwAnchorOccTarget$ of that target. A state
found in the first $4\%$ of the run and still short at the end is exactly the deficit mFR
claims to repair. The $\beta=8$ cells were rejected by the same rule despite lying nearer the
regime boundary: their gap of $\approx0.21$ falls below the $0.25$ threshold and they qualify
only through the below-half criterion at $\approx0.20$ against a $0.20$ threshold --- the
knife-edge the rule exists to exclude.

\subsection{Four arms, and what the sham controls}
\label{sec:gw_sham}
Every arm inside a seed shares initial conditions and Langevin noise, so all comparisons are
paired. Alongside ABF, oracle mFR (target from the analytic $F$; non-deployable) and
practical mFR (target from the online EMA of the bias), we run a \textbf{matched sham}: it
fires at the same event times and performs the \emph{same realised number} of clone and
delete events as the oracle arm --- copied from its partner row, not re-derived from its own
diverged state --- and differs only in drawing the identities uniformly at random. Across
$128$ paired comparisons the counts matched with $\GwShamMismatch$ mismatches.

This control has not existed anywhere in this study before. Every previous arm
(\texttt{fr\_estimated}, \texttt{fr\_uniform}, \texttt{fr\_oracle}, \texttt{fr\_active})
derives its replacement direction from the same Fisher--Rao score and differs only in how the
target is built, so the proposition ``the gain comes from balanced resampling rather than
from Fisher--Rao steering'' had never been tested against an arm that lacks the direction.

\subsection{A calibration run, then a confirmatory one}
\label{sec:gw_confirm}
The first pass at this anchor selected each arm's FR rate from a four-point ladder
\emph{and} reported that rate's confidence interval on the same runs. That is
tuning-selection bias: the interval is conditioned on having chosen the best-looking rung. We
treat that pass as calibration only. It fixed
$\gamma_{\mathrm{practical}} = \GwEstGamma$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{oracle}} = \GwOracleGamma$
--- the gentlest rates that pass the preregistered health gates
($\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{anc}}/N \ge \GwEssGate$, $w_{\max} \le \GwWmaxGate$) --- and those,
together with every other knob, the primary metric and the success rule, were frozen in a
committed preregistration before the confirmatory run began.

The confirmatory run uses $\GwcSeeds$ fresh seeds, disjoint from the calibration seeds, with
no ladder and no tuning. Its success rule, fixed in advance, required the practical arm to
reach a median $\le -10\%$ with a $95\%$ CI upper endpoint below $-5\%$ on at least $24$ of
$32$ seeds, with both health gates passing.

\paragraph{The attribution statistic.} Comparing each arm against ABF and then eyeballing the
two contrasts is weaker than comparing an arm against \emph{its own sham} directly on the same
seed, which holds the event schedule and the replacement count fixed by construction so that
only the selection direction differs. That contrast gives
\begin{equation*}
  \text{practical mFR vs its sham: } \GwcDirect\%\ [\GwcDirectCIlo, \GwcDirectCIhi],
  \quad \GwcDirectWins \text{ seeds},
  \qquad
  \text{oracle vs its sham: } \GwcOracleDirect\%\ [\GwcOracleDirectCIlo,
  \GwcOracleDirectCIhi].
\end{equation*}
Both intervals exclude zero. Separately, the practical sham is \textbf{\GwcShamTost} to ABF
by two one-sided tests against a $\pm\GwcMargin\%$ margin
($\GwcShamGain\%$, $90\%$ CI $[\GwcShamCIlo, \GwcShamCIhi]$).

\begin{remark}[Two statistical points, and two places this study got them wrong]
\label{rem:gw_verdict}
\emph{A directional label needs an interval that excludes zero.} The calibration analysis
labelled a sham cell an ``improvement'' on a median of $-1.1\%$ with an interval of
$[-11.1, +6.4]$, purely because the interval was wide. That reports the imprecision of the
estimate rather than the size of the effect; a directional call now requires the interval to
exclude zero, and anything else is equivalent or inconclusive.

\emph{``No interval excluded zero'' is not equivalence.} Failure to detect an effect is not
evidence that the effect is negligible, and the calibration write-up leaned on exactly that
phrasing. Equivalence is now a TOST against a predeclared margin. It matters: under the
correct test the \emph{oracle} arm's sham is \GwcShamOracleTost{}
($90\%$ CI $[\GwcShamOracleCIlo, \GwcShamOracleCIhi]$, whose lower end just clears the
margin), so only the practical arm's sham supports an equivalence claim. The headline rests
on the practical arm, and the oracle's is reported as inconclusive.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Three things the design was built to rule out}
\paragraph{It is establishment, not discovery.} In the mechanism control one walker starts
across the gateway, so discovery is handed to the sampler for free and any remaining
acceleration must be population establishment. Practical mFR still gives
$\GwcOneRight\%$ $[\GwcOneRightCIlo, \GwcOneRightCIhi]$ on $\GwcOneRightWins$ seeds with the
health gates passing. The effect survives when first passage is removed.

\paragraph{It is a better bias, not a better-behaved estimator.} $I_F$ is computed from the
same accumulators the FR mechanism perturbs, and birth--death makes replicas correlated
descendants, so a gain measured that way could in principle be a change in the estimator's
statistics rather than a genuinely better bias. We therefore freeze each arm's learned mean
force at the same physical time, launch a \emph{fresh} population --- identical across arms,
so no arm inherits an advantage from where its walkers ended up --- with no adaptation and no
birth--death, and reconstruct
$\widehat F = B - \beta^{-1}\log \hat p_B$ from the sampled density. On that
estimator-independent endpoint practical mFR gives $\GwcFrozen\%$
$[\GwcFrozenCIlo, \GwcFrozenCIhi]$: the same sign and essentially the same size as the online
number. The bias itself is better.

This endpoint also refines the attribution rather than merely confirming it. The practical
sham reads $\GwcShamFrozen\%$ $[\GwcShamFrozenCIlo, \GwcShamFrozenCIhi]$ here --- small, but
\emph{not} equivalent within the margin, unlike on the online endpoint. Uniform turnover does
improve the learned bias a little. The direct arm-versus-sham contrast on the same endpoint
is $\GwcDirectFrozen\%$ $[\GwcDirectFrozenCIlo, \GwcDirectFrozenCIhi]$, still excluding zero.
The honest summary is therefore a split rather than a clean dichotomy: generic turnover
contributes a minority of the benefit, and the Fisher--Rao direction contributes the majority.

\paragraph{The headline is not the largest available number.} At the rate
$\gamma = \GwFrozenGamma$ inherited from the accepted entropic-bottleneck setup, the
calibration gains are considerably larger --- $\GwFrozenOracleGain\%$ for the oracle and
$\GwFrozenEstGain\%$ for the practical arm --- but \emph{both health gates fail}
($\mathrm{ESS}_{\mathrm{anc}}/N = \GwFrozenOracleEss$ and $\GwFrozenEstEss$,
$w_{\max} = \GwFrozenOracleWmax$). The confirmatory rates are the gentlest that pass, not the
best-performing. This is the same accuracy--diversity boundary the WCA dimer exhibits
(\cref{sec:case_wca}), reached here from the other side.

\subsection{Why the regime exists at all: a two-state reduction}
\label{sec:gw_twostate}
The separation the gateway was built to create is not special to this potential. Coarse-grain
the reaction coordinate into ``not in the rare state'' and ``in it'', let $p(t)$ be the
population fraction of the rare state and $q$ its target mass, and write the mean-field
kinetics as
\begin{equation}
  \dot p = k_{01}(1-p) - k_{10}\,p ,
  \qquad
  p(\infty) = \frac{k_{01}}{k_{01}+k_{10}} = q .
  \label{eq:twostate}
\end{equation}
Two timescales follow, and they are not the same one.

\emph{Discovery} is a first-passage event among $N$ independent walkers, so it happens on
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{hit}} \sim \frac{1}{N k_{01}} ,
  \label{eq:thit}
\end{equation}
which is fast simply because $N$ is large: a rare crossing becomes likely once you have
enough tries. \emph{Establishment} is the relaxation of \cref{eq:twostate} toward $q$, whose
rate is the eigenvalue $k_{01}+k_{10}$ and which therefore takes
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{est}} \sim \frac{1}{k_{01}+k_{10}} ,
  \label{eq:test}
\end{equation}
with \emph{no factor of $N$}. Adding walkers buys discovery and does not buy establishment.
The ratio $T_{\mathrm{est}}/T_{\mathrm{hit}} \sim N k_{01}/(k_{01}+k_{10})$ grows with $N$, so
the establishment-limited window is not a curiosity of this model --- it is what a
many-walker sampler generically produces once $k_{01}$ is small, which is exactly the
condition under which the coordinate was worth biasing in the first place.

Marginal Fisher--Rao reallocation adds a term whose local linear rate near $q$ is set by the
resampling intensity $\gamma$ rather than by the physical crossing rate:
\begin{equation}
  \dot p = \underbrace{k_{01}(1-p) - k_{10}p}_{\text{physical transport}}
         \;+\; \underbrace{\gamma\,p\,\log\!\big(q/p\big)\,\big|_{\text{lin}}
               \approx -\gamma\,(p-q)}_{\text{reallocation}} ,
  \label{eq:fr_relax}
\end{equation}
so the relaxation rate becomes $k_{01}+k_{10}+\gamma$ while the fixed point stays at $q$
provided the target is correct. That single expression accounts for all four outcomes in this
study:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ABF-sufficient} (alanine, valine, the alkanes): $k_{01}+k_{10}$ is already
  large compared with $1/T$, so $\gamma$ changes a relaxation that had already finished.
  Adding it can only inject resampling noise --- which is what the neutral verdicts, and the
  mild harm at high rate, look like.
  \item \textbf{Establishment-limited} (the gateway, and \cref{sec:case_wca}):
  $N k_{01} \gg 1/T \gg k_{01}+k_{10}$. Discovery is done, relaxation is not, and $\gamma$
  supplies the missing rate. This is the regime, and \cref{eq:fr_relax} says it exists
  whenever the two inequalities can be satisfied together.
  \item \textbf{Discovery-limited} ($R_{15}$): $N k_{01} \lesssim 1/T$, so $p(t) = 0$ over the
  whole run. The reallocation term is proportional to $p$ and vanishes identically --- mFR
  cannot create a representative of a state that has none. No choice of $\gamma$ changes
  this, which is why the $R_{15}$ rate ladder is monotone in the wrong direction.
  \item \textbf{The cost}, and why $\gamma$ cannot simply be raised: the term in
  \cref{eq:fr_relax} moves \emph{copies}, so the ancestor diversity decays at a rate that
  also scales with $\gamma$. Accuracy improves with $\gamma$ while the effective sample size
  the mean-force estimator consumes decays with it, which is the accuracy--diversity boundary
  seen in both the WCA rate ladder and this study's own health-gate failures at
  $\gamma = \GwFrozenGamma$.
\end{itemize}
The reduction is deliberately crude --- it assumes a single rare state, mean-field kinetics
and a correct target --- but it is enough to say why the three regimes are three and not a
continuum, and why the boundary between them moves with $N$ and with $T$ rather than with the
shape of the potential.

\begin{remark}[The deployable target captures the gain here]
\label{rem:gw_deployable}
In the entropy-dominant stress test (\cref{sec:eb_entropy_dominant}) the oracle target
diverged sharply from the online estimate, and the deployable arm captured essentially none
of the oracle's benefit. At this anchor the two coincide: the direct-versus-sham contrasts are
$\GwcDirect\%$ for the practical arm and $\GwcOracleDirect\%$ for the oracle. In terms of
\cref{eq:fr_relax}, both targets place the fixed point close enough to $q$ that what matters
is $\gamma$, not the residual target error; the oracle buys shape information the
reallocation here does not need. Target quality matters in proportion to how much shape
information the reallocation actually requires, which is small when the instruction is
``move mass across the gateway'' and large when it is ``reproduce an entropy-dominated
profile''.
\end{remark}
