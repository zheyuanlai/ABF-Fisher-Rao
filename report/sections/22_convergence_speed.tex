\section{Convergence speed: one endpoint across every benchmark}
\label{sec:convergence}

Every case study so far has been read as a verdict --- does mFR help here, yes or no. That is
the right question for a regime map, but it discards the shape of the run. Each core in this
study already scores
\begin{equation}
  e_F(t) \;=\; \big\| \widehat F_t - F_{\mathrm{ref}} - c_t \big\|_{\Ltwo(\Omega_{\mathrm{eval}})},
  \qquad
  c_t \;=\; \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{c}\, \big\| \widehat F_t - F_{\mathrm{ref}} - c \big\|,
  \label{eq:conv_eF}
\end{equation}
at every saved checkpoint and stores it per seed, because the preregistered primary endpoint
$I_F=\int_0^T e_F(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$ is built from it. What the study has never done is put those
curves side by side. This section does, and asks the question the time integral answers only
implicitly: \emph{does the Fisher--Rao correction make the free energy converge faster?}

Nothing here re-runs dynamics. The atlas is a re-reading of stored artifacts, produced by
\texttt{scripts/extract\_convergence\_atlas.py} and analysed by
\texttt{scripts/analyze\_convergence\_atlas.py}.

\begin{remark}[Sign convention]
This section uses the \emph{relative change} convention of the closure tables throughout:
a \emph{negative} percentage means the error fell, i.e. mFR is better. It does not use the
gain convention of \cref{sec:synthesis}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{One convention, and what makes the comparison admissible}
\label{sec:conv_convention}

Each system's core removes the arbitrary additive constant on its own evaluation window and
reports an interior-window RMS error; these definitions were checked against one another and
agree up to endpoint weighting across the WCA, gateway, entropic-bottleneck, alkane and
alanine implementations. The \emph{vertical} scales are therefore not comparable between
panels --- different systems, collective variables and windows --- and nothing in this section
pools across them. What is comparable is the shape of each curve and each panel's own
arm-to-arm contrast.

Two provenance facts decide which artifacts may be read. First, the WCA production tree of
\cref{sec:case_wca} was scored against the reference later found wrong
(\cref{sec:reference_correction}) and retains no profile time series, so its curves can be neither
reused nor rescored; the two stored references differ by up to $0.061\,k_BT$ inside the
evaluation window. The WCA panels here therefore read the confirmatory Case~IX and five-arm
trees, whose runs store the full $\widehat F_t$ series and carry the corrected high-precision
reference in the file. Second, that stored series reproduces the stored $e_F(t)$ \emph{exactly}
(maximum absolute difference $0$ over all $160$ runs) when recomputed from
$\widehat F_t$ and the in-file reference on the $[-0.1,1.1]$ window, so the extraction is not
silently applying a different window or reference.

The same check is available end-to-end: re-deriving the primary endpoint from the atlas
returns $\CvWcaInt\%$ for WCA against the shipped $\WcvEstGain\%$, $\CvGwInt\%$ for the
gateway, and $\CvFiveInt\%$ and $\CvFiveCount\%$ for the five-arm contrast --- the shipped
verdicts, to the digits they are quoted at. An extraction that had drifted would not land on
them.

The tally is $\CvNFaster$ faster, $\CvNSame$ no difference and $\CvNSlower$ significantly
slower, over $\CvNCells$ distinct systems and cells. The convergence reading therefore does
not overturn the regime map of \cref{sec:synthesis}; it restates it on a finer endpoint and
sharpens the conditional. Where ABF develops a persistent population deficit the free energy
converges materially faster; where ABF already resolves the coordinate the curves lie on top
of each other; where the bottleneck is discovery rather than representation
($R_{15}$, \cref{sec:cv_harder_1d}) reallocation cannot act and the curve is unmoved.

\begin{remark}[Equal compute is automatic in these panels]
Birth--death reallocates existing replicas at fixed population size, and $N$ and the number of
steps are identical across arms in every panel of \cref{fig:conv_atlas}. No arm buys its curve
with extra force evaluations, so the obvious objection to a speed claim --- that the faster
method was simply given more work --- does not arise here, independently of the fixed-budget
study of \cref{sec:wca_equal_compute}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Time to a prescribed accuracy}
\label{sec:conv_tau}

A percentage change in a time integral is not what a practitioner buys. The direct quantity is
\begin{equation}
  \tau_\varepsilon \;=\; \inf\Big\{\, t \;:\; e_F(s)\le\varepsilon
     \ \ \forall\, s\in[t,\min(t+\Delta,T)] \,\Big\},
  \qquad
  S_\varepsilon \;=\; \frac{\tau_\varepsilon(\mathrm{ABF})}{\tau_\varepsilon(\mathrm{mFR})},
  \label{eq:conv_tau}
\end{equation}
with a persistence window $\Delta = \CvPersist\,T$ that prevents a single lucky excursion
below the threshold from being scored as convergence.

The thresholds are fixed by a rule declared before any arm is inspected, because choosing
$\varepsilon$ after seeing the curves would let the analyst choose the answer. We use
$\varepsilon = f\,e_0$ for $f\in\{1/2,1/4,1/8\}$, where $e_0$ is the error at $t=0$ --- a point
at which every arm carries an identically zero bias, so $e_0$ is a property of the system and
not of the method. A fourth threshold is ABF's own median final error, which asks directly how
much simulation an arm needs to reach the answer ABF finishes with. Where a threshold is never
met, $\tau_\varepsilon=\infty$ is reported as censored and drawn at the axis edge; dropping
those cases would convert ``this arm never got there'' into ``this arm is not in the average''.

Where the effect is real it is large. To reach ABF's final accuracy, mFR needs
$1/\CvEbEightSpeedup$ of the budget at $\beta=8$, $1/\CvEbTwelveSpeedup$ at $\beta=12$ and
$1/\CvWcaSpeedup$ on the starved WCA cell. \Cref{fig:conv_speedup} shows both endpoints
together.

\subsection{What kind of acceleration: a rate, or a head start?}
\label{sec:conv_kind}

A curve can lie below another for two quite different reasons --- a faster decay rate, or an
approach to a lower asymptotic floor --- and the distinction decides what may be claimed. The
two positive systems answer it differently, which is the most consequential finding of this
section.

On the starved WCA cell the ratio $e_F^{\mathrm{mFR}}(t)/e_F^{\mathrm{ABF}}(t)$ falls
monotonically to $0.54$ at $t=220$ of $240$ and never returns above unity; the final-time
advantage, $\CvWcaFinal\%$, is larger than the integrated one, so integrating from $t=0$
\emph{dilutes} the effect. On the gateway anchor the same ratio bottoms at $0.37$ at $t=3.0$,
crosses back above unity at $t=16.6$, and ends at $1.11$: mFR wins the integrated endpoint on
$\CvGwWins$ of $\CvGwSeeds$ seeds and \emph{loses} the final-time endpoint by $\CvGwFinal\%$ on
all of them. \Cref{fig:conv_mechanism}(c) draws the two ratios together.

  \textbf{(b)} The gateway anchor, with the mFR/ABF error ratio inset. \textbf{(c)} The two

The frozen-bias controls, which discard the online accumulators and re-evaluate the learned
bias in a fresh non-adapting run, do not settle the question either --- they disagree with the
online endpoints, and in the gateway they disagree in sign. Both columns below are read from the
\emph{same} run tree per row --- the replicate for the gateway, so that the frozen and online
figures are not a ratio taken across two batches:

The WCA frozen figure is the reconstruction of \cref{sec:wca_frozen} rescored against the
corrected reference ($\CvWcaFrozenAbf \to \CvWcaFrozenFr$; it reads $\CvWcaFrozenOld\%$ at the
superseded one, so the reference correction is not what drives it). It keeps the sign of the
online result and loses an order of magnitude in size. Two caveats bound how hard this can be
pushed: the frozen tree runs at $N=512$ against the confirmatory tree's $N=1024$, and the
frozen reconstruction carries its own error floor of $\CvWcaFrozenAbf$ against an online final
error near $0.09$, so it is a blunt instrument for resolving a difference of this size.

We therefore claim finite-time acceleration, and specifically do \emph{not} claim an improved
asymptotic rate. Reporting an exponential-decay fit here would put a rate constant on data that
does not identify one separately from the floor.

\subsection{Is the acceleration Fisher--Rao-specific?}
\label{sec:conv_specific}

\Cref{sec:wca_q1} already answers this on the WCA cell: at matched turnover, count balancing
returns $\CvFiveCount\%$ against mFR's $\CvFiveInt\%$ and recovers the whole gain, while the
Laplacian rule is inert at $\CvFiveBook\%$. \Cref{fig:conv_mechanism}(a) shows what that
equivalence looks like as a curve rather than as an interval: the two are not merely
statistically indistinguishable at the endpoint, they are superimposed throughout.

The convergence reading adds a second, independent instance of the same result, in a system
where it had not previously been read as a mechanism test. At $\beta=8$ in the entropic
bottleneck, the \emph{uniform} target --- which carries no Fisher--Rao construction at all, and
simply pushes the occupancy toward flat --- gives $\CvAblUni\%$ against the estimated target's
$\CvAblEst\%$, capturing most of the gain on $\CvAblUniWins/\CvAblUniSeeds$ seeds. The
\emph{oracle} target, which is handed the exact free energy, is $\CvAblOra\%$ --- worse than
ABF. The same ordering appears on the gateway, where the oracle arm trails the estimated one.
If the geometry were the operative ingredient, the arm holding the exact target would be the
ceiling; across the study it is the ceiling in one cell only. This is consistent with the
structural result of \cref{prop:sel_invariance} and with the conclusion already reached in
\cref{sec:wca_q1}: what the mechanism supplies is directed reallocation, and Fisher--Rao is one
principled way to specify the direction rather than the source of the benefit.

\subsection{The one dose--response in the study}
\label{sec:conv_dose}

Single contrasts can be explained away; a monotone dose--response is much harder to. The FR
rate ladder at $\beta=8$ in the entropic bottleneck spans a factor of $50$ in $\gamma$ and the
integrated error falls monotonically across all of it:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$\gamma$ & $1$ & $3$ & $5$ & $10$ & $15$ & $25$ & $50$ \\
$\Delta I_F$ [\%] & $\CvDoseOne$ & $\CvDoseThree$ & $\CvDoseFive$ & $\CvDoseTen$ &
  $\CvDoseFifteen$ & $\CvDoseTwentyFive$ & $\CvDoseFifty$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

with every seed improving from $\gamma=10$ upward. Two properties make this the strongest
single piece of evidence in the section. The cell quoted elsewhere in this report,
$\gamma=15$, is \emph{not} the maximum of the ladder, so it is not a best-of-seven selection.
And the response is ordered in the knob that controls the amount of reallocation, which is
what a mechanistic explanation predicts and what a selection artifact does not.

It also sets a boundary the study has not characterised. In the alkanes, raising the FR rate
\emph{harms} --- the aggressive pentane arms of \cref{sec:case_pentane} and
\cref{sec:cv_harder_1d} degrade monotonically with rate, and in $R_{15}$ they collapse lineage
diversity. Two systems therefore respond to the same knob in opposite directions, and nothing
in this report locates the crossover.

\subsection{What the atlas does not support}
\label{sec:conv_limits}

\paragraph{The metastability panel is not evidence.}
Each FR arm in \cref{sec:case_meta} was selected from $\NFRconfigs$ configurations
while ABF was run at exactly one, so its $\CvToyInt\%$ is a best-of-$\NFRconfigs$ contrasted
against a best-of-one; its interval, $[\CvToyCIlo, \CvToyCIhi]$ on $\CvToySeeds$ seeds, spans
zero. \Cref{fig:conv_toy} draws all $\NFRconfigs$ curves: the cloud straddles ABF and the
selected configuration sits at its favourable edge. The panel is retained in
\cref{fig:conv_atlas} with that reading, rather than dropped, because the asymmetry is a
property of the design and is better shown than hidden.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{fig_conv_04_toy_selection.png}
  \caption{The tuning asymmetry in the metastability model. Faint lines are the
  $\NFRconfigs-1$ estimated-target configurations that were \emph{not} selected; the heavy
  blue line is the one that was, and the black line is the single ABF configuration it is
  compared against. The spread is the tuning budget, not a method effect.}
  \label{fig:conv_toy}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Every positive is a coarse-grained or constructed system.}
The four accelerated cells are the WCA dimer, the entropic bottleneck at two temperatures, and
the gateway --- one condensed-phase model potential and two designed models. Every atomistic
system in this report is either a null (butane, pentane on $\varphi_1$, the torsion torus, both
dipeptides) or was stopped ABF-sufficient before an mFR arm was licensed (methane, sodium
chloride, valine). The claim available today is therefore about a regime, demonstrated in
models built or found to exhibit it, and not about molecular free-energy calculation in
general.

\paragraph{The largest effects carry the weakest controls.}
The WCA and gateway positives each have a matched sham --- the same number of clone/delete
events at the same times with direction randomised. The entropic bottleneck, which supplies the
two largest numbers in \cref{tab:conv_scoreboard}, has \emph{no} turnover control at any
$\beta$. The uniform-target ablation of \cref{sec:conv_specific} bounds the role of the target
but not the role of resampling itself, so the first question a reader will ask of a $\CvEbEightInt\%$
gain from a birth--death scheme --- whether undirected resampling at the same intensity would do
as well --- is not answered for that system. A matched sham at $\beta=8$ and $\beta=12$ is the
single highest-value addition to this section and is listed as such in
\cref{sec:conclusion}.
